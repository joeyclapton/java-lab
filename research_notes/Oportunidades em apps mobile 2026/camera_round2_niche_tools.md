# Camera / photo / video apps, round 2: niche capture tools, event cameras, small utilities, named apps, AI video costs

Method note (read first). State as of 13 November 2026. This pass used the full budget of 35 web searches and 2 page fetches. Page fetching is blocked for most hosts, so almost every figure below comes from **search-result summaries, not from pages that were opened**; only two pages were read directly (Apple's `ImageCreator` documentation and the `apple/ml-stable-diffusion` README). Where a search summary did not say which of several result URLs a figure came from, the most likely URL is linked and the item is marked "(URL attribution approximate)". Third-party trackers disagree systematically: AppGoblin reports revenue only in coarse bands ("<US$10K", "US$10K+", "US$50K+" per month) and is routinely 4x to 40x below Adapty's paywall library and ScreensDesign for the same app. ScreensDesign figures are labelled monthly on some pages and carry no period on others. Treat every tracker figure as an order-of-magnitude signal. Nothing here repeats the first pass (CapCut, cleaners, Remini/Picsart, Halide, mood.camera, Dazz, Sora/Lapse/Nano Banana, iOS camera APIs, image-generation costs, POV headline).

## 1. Niche capture tools: named apps, revenue/download estimates, pricing, team size

### Takeaway
Paying demand is visible in four capture niches: creator teleprompters (BIGVU at roughly US$300-400K a month on two trackers; a solo developer living off Teleprompter Pro since 2018), 3D/LiDAR scanning (Polycam about US$6.5M gross in 2023 with nearly 100K paid subscribers), spare-phone baby/security cameras (Annie about US$40K a month on one tracker; AlfredCamera US$2-5M a year) and photo scanning (Photomyne, US$17M in 2025). Pro video capture is given away free by Blackmagic and Apple, spatial/Vision Pro capture is tiny, and passport-photo and most baby-monitor clones sit under US$10K a month. No revenue data at all was found for timelapse, long-exposure, astrophotography, dashcam, multi-cam/streaming or real-estate HDR apps.

### Inferences
- Rough ranking of niche capture types by evidence of paying demand (largest first): creator teleprompter-plus-captions (hundreds of thousands of dollars a month at the top, on two of three trackers), 3D/room scanning (mid single-digit millions a year for the leader), spare-phone monitoring (tens of thousands a month for the best baby monitor; low millions a year for AlfredCamera), then passport photo, spatial video and long-exposure (single-digit thousands a month or unknown).
- Teleprompter is the one niche here with documented solo-developer livelihoods (Teleprompter Pro since 2018; Sufler at about US$2.4-3.8K MRR). The long tail is large (dozens of near-identical "Teleprompter for Video" listings), which implies heavy App Store search competition; BIGVU's lead comes from bundling captions and editing with the prompter and pricing up to US$139.99 a year.
- Baby monitors support unusually high price points for a utility (a US$149.99 lifetime tier), but the Flippa listings show what an undifferentiated clone earns: a few hundred dollars a month.
- Free first-party and free pro tools cap several niches: Blackmagic Camera and Final Cut Camera (pro video), Continuity Camera (webcam), Polycam's 2026 free room scanning (basic LiDAR/floor-plan capture). A paid app in these niches must sell workflow, not capture quality; Kino's US$9.99 one-time price shows where the price ceiling sits for a paid pro video camera.
- Floor-plan and scanning value accrued to B2B buyers (CubiCasa sold to a valuation company; Polycam moved to an enterprise platform), which suggests the consumer app alone is not the business.

### Gaps
- No revenue, download or pricing estimates were retrieved for: Teleprompter Premium+ (by name), timelapse/hyperlapse apps, Spectre, AstroShader or other astrophotography apps, dashcam apps, Cloud Baby Monitor, pet-camera apps, Scaniverse, magicplan, real-estate HDR/listing-photo apps, document scanners, Detail, Prism Live or other multi-cam/streaming apps. One search each for dashcam and long-exposure returned no usable numbers; the others were cut by the search budget.
- No Appfigures or Sensor Tower figure was retrieved for any app in this section except Polycam and Blackmagic Camera (both 2024). Everything else rests on Adapty, ScreensDesign and AppGoblin, which disagree widely.
- Team sizes were found only for Polycam (about 48), AlfredCamera (27, or 51-200), CubiCasa (about 60) and Pixelcut (about 5-11).
- Unverified leads from the researcher's background knowledge (no source retrieved; verify before use): "Teleprompter Premium+" is believed to be Joe Allen's app, i.e. the same product family as Teleprompter Pro; Scaniverse is believed to be owned by Niantic and free; magicplan is believed to be made by Sensopia (Montreal) with tiered monthly subscriptions.

## 2. Event and shared cameras: pricing per event, revenue signals, acquisition, web versus App Clip versus native

### Takeaway
The host-paid event camera is real but already crowded and price-compressed: at least a dozen QR gallery products sell one-time packages of roughly US$29-99 per event, several have permanent free tiers, and new entrants were still launching in 2026. Operators report large usage (GUESTPIX 150,000 events; PixelParty 30,000 weddings; Guestlense 10,000 weddings) but none discloses revenue, and POV's revenue remains unresolved between under US$10K and about US$200K a month.

### Cited Findings
- POV pricing is per event and scales with guest count: one competitor comparison lists events up to 10 guests free, US$4.99 for up to 25 guests, rising to US$90 for up to 250 guests; another competitor says users typically report about US$25-50+ per event and that the price is shown only at checkout; POV's own pricing page is a calculator by event type and guest count (competitor-reported figures, mid-2026; they conflict). — [Knipsmig, "real prices, verified July 2026"](https://knipsmig.com/best/disposable-camera-apps); [Knipsmig, POV pricing](https://knipsmig.com/pov-camera-pricing); [Fotify vs POV, 2026](https://fotify.app/blog/fotify-vs-pov-2026/); [POV pricing calculator](https://pov.camera/pricing)
- POV guests do not install an app: POV's own machine-readable site summary says guests "don't need to download an app", and comparison sites describe the mechanism as an iOS App Clip or browser flow opened from a QR code; the host uses the native app, which is free with in-app purchases (URL attribution approximate for the App Clip description). — [pov.camera/llms.txt](https://pov.camera/llms.txt); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1636032890?l=en-us); [Knipsmig](https://knipsmig.com/alternatives/pov-camera)
- POV on Android: 1M installs and about 46.2K monthly active users (AppGoblin third-party estimate, undated). — [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.untitledshows.pov)
- Acquisition channel: a founder-playbook summary of a RevenueCat Sub Club episode (Joseph Choi on viral consumer apps) says POV spread through wedding-story short videos in which the product reveal is embedded in the story, including a "rule" that guests had to use the app (third-party commentary, not POV's own statement). — [BetterLaunch playbook](https://www.betterlaunch.co/playbooks/joseph-choi-viral-app-founders-10)
- Flat one-time prices at web-based QR galleries (2026 roundups, mostly written by competitors): Wedibox free, US$29, US$49 or US$79 (the US$79 plan adds RSVPs, seating chart and a wedding website on the same QR code); Kululu free, US$39 or US$99 (against a US$199 list price); GuestCam US$49 or US$97 with an optional US$45 face-search add-on; GuestPix from US$39 with a three-month upload window, a free tier of 50 photos for 30 days, and a top bundle covering three events; Guestlense US$49 digital or US$99 with 30 printed QR cards; Joy free. — [Wedibox comparison](https://www.wedibox.com/compare/best-wedding-photo-sharing-apps); [Wedibox vs GuestPix](https://www.wedibox.com/compare/guestpix); [Fotify roundup, 2026](https://fotify.app/blog/best-wedding-photo-sharing-apps-2026/); [iTechGuides, GuestPix](https://www.itechguides.com/products/guestpix/); [Yogile roundup](https://www.yogile.com/blog/best-wedding-photo-sharing-app)
- Cheaper and free alternatives exist: Gather Shot lists flat fees of US$59.99 and US$99.99 and argues a 200-guest wedding costs more on POV; Fotify has a free tier of 50 photos with 7 days of gallery access; Knipsmig describes itself as completely free with a one-month storage limit. — [Gather Shot vs POV](https://gathershot-marketing.pages.dev/compare/gather-shot-vs-pov/); [Fotify](https://fotify.app/wedding-photo-sharing-app/); [Knipsmig pricing](https://knipsmig.com/pricing)
- Self-reported scale: GUESTPIX (Australia, began as a side project) says it has been used at more than 150,000 events in 100 countries; PixelParty claims more than 30,000 weddings (its Trustpilot page includes complaints that guests could not upload); Guestlense, started from one couple's own wedding, says it now powers more than 10,000 weddings and sells a QR-card customiser with printed cards; Wedibox was built in 2022 by a wedding photographer. — [GUESTPIX](https://guestpix.com/); [Trustpilot, PixelParty](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/pixelparty.site); [Munaluchi Bride](https://www.munaluchibridal.com/guestlense-wedding-photo-sharing-app-story/); [Wedibox](https://www.wedibox.com/features/wedding-photo-sharing)
- New entrants continue: Our Event Album (Sydney) launched QR photo sharing in February 2026 and claims more than 1,000 hosts; software directories list further products (EventSnap, Momentzy, InvitePix, GuestReel, Wedding Arrivals, GuestQR, QRPict). — [PR.com](https://www.pr.com/press-release/960288); [GetApp, EventSnap](https://www.getapp.com/all-software/a/eventsnap-1/); [Capterra, InvitePix](https://www.capterra.com/p/10041959/InvitePix/); [Capterra, GuestReel](https://www.capterra.com/p/10182444/GuestReel/)
- Kululu's marketing claims that only about 5% of the photos and videos guests take at weddings reach the couple (vendor claim, not independent data). — [Kululu](https://www.kululu.com/wedding-photo-sharing-app)

### Inferences
- Two delivery models coexist: (a) native host app plus App Clip/browser for guests with a disposable-camera mechanic and delayed reveal (POV), priced by guest count; (b) pure web apps with a QR code and flat one-time packages (Wedibox, Kululu, GuestPix, GuestCam, Guestlense). The web products acquire customers through search and comparison pages (every vendor publishes "X vs Y" and "best wedding photo apps" pages), while POV's reported channel is short-form wedding video.
- If the self-reported volumes are roughly right, GUESTPIX's 150,000 events at US$39 or more implies cumulative gross revenue in the millions of dollars, and Guestlense's 10,000 weddings at US$49-99 implies roughly US$0.5-1M cumulative. These are arithmetic illustrations from vendor claims, not measured revenue.
- Price pressure is toward zero for the basic gallery (free tiers at Wedibox, Kululu, Joy, Fotify, Knipsmig). Paid differentiation in the sources comes from bundling (RSVP, seating chart, website), physical goods (printed QR cards), AI face search (GuestCam's US$45 add-on) and longer storage windows.
- A solo developer entering in late 2026 would be a late entrant in English-language weddings; the sources show no entrant differentiated by region, language or event type, which is consistent with (but does not prove) room for a localized or vertical version.

### Gaps
- No revenue, MRR or profit figure was found for any event-camera or QR-gallery operator; a dedicated extended search for founder posts (Indie Hackers, bootstrapped stories) returned none. POV's revenue conflict from the first pass (about US$200K a month on ScreensDesign versus under US$10K on AppGoblin) is unresolved.
- No evidence was found on B2B2C distribution (planners, venues, photographers), on corporate-event demand, or on conversion from guest to future host.
- Lense: no reliable pricing or revenue was found; one review site criticises its "mystery pricing". "Guestlense" (US$49-99) may be a different product.
- A Playkit newsletter page returned for the POV query cites 2.3M+ views, 177K+ likes and 25.6K+ saves for the app's content, but the page URL refers to Dispo, so it is unclear which app the figures describe; excluded. A founder interview about an app called "ONCE" (US$20K monthly revenue within 83 days) also surfaced without enough context to tell whether it is an event camera; excluded.
- Shared-album and group-camera apps other than wedding galleries were not searched.

## 3. Small photo utilities: who leads, how much they earn, how much is ASO-driven

- On-device generation does not currently offer a zero-cost substitute for cloud models in a third-party iPhone app: Apple's headless API is deprecated, the remaining Image Playground route is Apple's own sheet UI and style set, and self-shipped Core ML diffusion costs gigabytes of download and many seconds per image for 2022-era model quality. ## 6. Evidence of demand for the candidate gaps (host-paid event cameras, job-specific capture, one-time "look" cameras, creator tools, regional aesthetics)

### Takeaway
The strongest demand evidence is for job-specific capture sold to businesses (CompanyCam: US$415M raised at a US$2B valuation in August 2025 on roughly US$68M of estimated revenue) and for creator talking-head tooling (Captions US$28.4M trailing in-app revenue; BIGVU about US$300-400K a month). Host-paid event cameras show demand through operators' usage claims but no disclosed revenue and visible price compression. For one-time-purchase "look" cameras and regional aesthetics, this pass found no positive demand evidence, only weak or contradictory tracker estimates.

### Inferences
- Summary judgement by product type, from this pass's evidence (demand / competition):
  - Creator teleprompter plus captions: demand high and proven at several scales (solo to venture-backed); competition high, with a US$175M-funded leader and a long search-driven tail. A solo entry needs a narrower job (for example a specific profession, language or recording setup).
  - Job-specific capture for a trade (documentation, scanning, listing media): demand proven at B2B scale; competition is by vertical, and winners tend to be absorbed into workflow platforms. Feasible for a solo developer only in a small vertical the large players ignore; sales are B2B and slower.
  - Host-paid event camera: demand real, prices of US$29-99 per event accepted; competition already dense in English with free tiers. Viable mainly with regional, language or event-type focus, or with a bundled physical or service product.
  - Spare-phone baby/pet/security camera: demand real with high willingness to pay per user; clones earn hundreds of dollars a month; reliability and trust are the product, which is costly for one person to support.
  - Passport photo, compressors, GIF, vault clones: demand exists but is thin per app (mostly under US$10K a month) and search-ranked; outcome depends on ASO and paid search.
  - One-time-purchase "look" camera: no evidence of meaningful revenue outside established brands in this pass; consistent with the first pass's "default-fail without a distribution edge" reading.
  - Spatial/Vision Pro capture and pro video capture: small installed base in the first case, free first-party tools in the second; weakest candidates.
- The products with the best revenue per employee in the evidence (Aragon with 11 people, Photomyne with 22, Teleprompter Pro with one) each solve one concrete job with an obvious payer, and none depends on an aesthetic trend.

### Gaps
- No direct demand measurements were retrieved for any candidate gap: no search-volume, keyword or review-mining data, no surveys, and no conversion or retention benchmarks specific to these niches.
- Regional aesthetics and regional leaders were not researched (budget exhausted): nothing on Korea, Japan, China, Brazil or other local camera styles, or on localized event-camera or teleprompter products. Captions' 75% non-US revenue share is the only regional signal found.
- No evidence was found on what share of event-camera buyers are professional planners, venues or photographers versus individual hosts, or on corporate-event budgets.
- No sales data for any one-time-purchase camera (Kino, NightCap, Slow Shutter Cam, Spatialify) was found, so the claim that one-time "look" cameras lack demand rests on absence of evidence plus weak tracker estimates.
- Timestamp/GPS-stamp field cameras (for example Timemark) were searched together with CompanyCam and returned nothing.
