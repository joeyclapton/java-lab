# Build-and-run side of small consumer iOS apps, 2025-2026 (second pass: stack, running costs, AI unit costs, retention levers, pricing, distribution details)

Method note (read first). State as of 13 November 2026. All 35 permitted web searches were used. Page fetching worked only for developer.apple.com and github.com; the two permitted attempts on other hosts (expo.dev, revenuecat.com) both failed to resolve. So: items tagged **[Apple, fetched]** or **[GitHub, fetched]** were read on the page; everything else comes from **search-result summaries**, and where a result set contained several pages the attribution of a figure to one specific URL follows the search tool's summary and was not confirmed by opening the page. Each finding is tagged as Apple primary, vendor data, third-party estimate, secondary blog or self-reported. Nothing from background knowledge is in the findings. This pass does not repeat what `growth_monetization.md` already established.

## 1. Tech stack for small teams in 2026 (SwiftUI vs React Native + Expo vs Flutter vs Kotlin Multiplatform; AI coding tools; Liquid Glass / iOS 26-27 API access)

### Takeaway
No stack is disqualified: React Native + Expo now reaches Liquid Glass, native tabs, widgets and Live Activities, and RevenueCat's 2026 data shows React Native apps earning more per install than native or Flutter apps (correlation, not cause). The clear laggard on iOS-native look is Flutter, whose Liquid Glass issue has been open at low priority since June 2025; and the newest Apple-only benefits (free Private Cloud Compute inference, on-device model, Xcode 27 agents) arrive first, and natively only, in Swift.

### Cited Findings

**Adoption and monetization by framework**
- **RevenueCat 2026, revenue per install by framework [vendor data, via search summary].** Median day-14 revenue per install: React Native $0.34, native $0.22, Flutter $0.19. Median day-60: React Native $0.51, native $0.31, Flutter $0.29 (about 65% and 76% more). Median first-year value per payer: React Native $31.78 versus about $20-21 for native and Flutter. RevenueCat states the data "does not suggest that React Native is a prerequisite for strong monetization". The 2025 edition had different values (day-14 React Native $0.44; year-1 $22.80), so editions should not be mixed. — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/why-react-native-apps-make-more-money); [RevenueCat report page](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps); vendor framing in [Expo blog](https://expo.dev/blog/best-way-to-build-a-mobile-app-that-makes-money-in-2026)
- **Appfigures, established apps (December 2025) [third-party estimate, relayed by secondary roundups].** React Native detected in 1,350 of the top 10,000 non-game iOS apps versus 1,184 for Flutter; React Native also ahead on download and revenue share in the same table. Original Appfigures publication not retrieved. — [Quash roundup](https://quashbugs.com/blog/flutter-vs-react-native-statistics); [MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/react-native-vs-flutter/)

- **Apptopia (2024) [third-party estimate, unverified secondary].** Flutter "nearly 30%" of new free iOS apps in 2024, up from about 10% in 2021. Not confirmed against Apptopia. — [Programming Helper](https://www.programming-helper.com/tech/flutter-2026-cross-platform-mobile-leader-python)

### Inferences
- For an iOS-first app that leans on system surfaces (Liquid Glass chrome, widgets, Live Activities, Foundation Models, Private Cloud Compute), SwiftUI has the shortest path and no bridge maintenance; Expo is a close second for those surfaces as of SDK 56-58; Flutter is the weakest fit for an iOS-native look in 2026.
- The RevenueCat revenue-per-install gap in favour of React Native most plausibly reflects who chooses it (web-experienced, marketing-led teams, web-to-app funnels), not the framework. It does show that cross-platform is no monetization handicap.
- AI agents now write most of the code in either stack (agents are built into Xcode 27; Expo + Claude/Codex is a documented indie workflow). What the reports say they do not replace: real-device testing, product decisions, and knowledge of StoreKit, concurrency and review rules. Prompt-to-app web builders do not produce a store-ready native binary; Rork is the only one described as doing so.
- Apple's annual SDK step (Xcode 26 by April 2026, next step April 2027) plus new device classes means a cross-platform stack must be kept on a current SDK release every year; that maintenance is a real cost for a 1-3 person team.

- Stacks of specific indie hits (Cal AI, Quittr, HabitKit and similar): the dedicated search returned nothing verifiable.
- RevenueCat's share of apps by framework and any indie-only split: in the 330-page PDF, not retrieved.
- Kotlin Multiplatform adoption among consumer subscription apps: no data found.
- Access to Apple's Foundation Models / Private Cloud Compute from React Native or Flutter (bridges, packages): not researched.
- Stack Overflow 2025/2026 mobile framework percentages: not confirmed on the survey site.
- Measured build-speed gains from Cursor, Claude Code or Codex on mobile projects: only anecdotes found.

## 2. Backend and infrastructure costs (free tiers, typical bills, subscription tooling, analytics, crash reporting, fixed monthly total)

### Takeaway
A solo subscription app can run for roughly the Apple fee alone (about $8 a month) until it has real traction, and for well under $100 a month on paid entry tiers; the first cost that scales is the subscription platform's ~1% of tracked revenue, then database compute and egress. No reliable published bill for 10k or 100k monthly users was found, so the larger tiers have to be modelled from list prices.

### Cited Findings

**Apple fixed costs**
- **Developer Program [Apple, fetched].** "The Apple Developer Program annual fee is 99 USD". The page mentions a fee waiver only for accredited educational institutions and says students get no discount. — [Apple enrollment support](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/)
- **Commission [Apple, fetched].** Small Business Program: 15% on paid apps and in-app purchases for developers with up to $1M proceeds in the prior calendar year (and new developers). Standard subscriptions: 70% to developer in year one, 85% after one year of paid service. China mainland: 25% standard and 12% for Small Business Program and post-year-one renewals from 15 March 2026. Taiwan: 26% standard from 31 October 2026. — [Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/); [Subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/); [Apple developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/)
- **CloudKit [Apple, fetched].** "get up to 1PB of storage for your app's public data"; private data is stored "in your users' iCloud accounts for limitless scale as your user base grows". No price list on the page. Apple-Hosted Background Assets: 200 GB per app. — [CloudKit](https://developer.apple.com/icloud/cloudkit/); [App Store what's new](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/whats-new/)

**Backend platforms**
- **Supabase quotas [GitHub, fetched].** Free: 2 projects, 50,000 monthly active users, 500 MB database per project, 5 GB egress, 1 GB file storage, 500,000 edge-function invocations, 200 realtime peak connections. Pro: 100,000 MAU, 8 GB disk per project, 250 GB egress, 100 GB storage, 2 million edge-function invocations included, then usage-billed. — [Supabase docs source](https://github.com/supabase/supabase/blob/master/apps/docs/content/guides/platform/billing-on-supabase.mdx)
- **Supabase prices [secondary, checked against vendor page Sept-Nov 2026 per the sources].** Pro $25/month; overage $0.00325 per MAU and $0.125 per GB-month of database; compute billed by tier less a $10 credit; free projects pause after a week of inactivity. — [Jet Admin guide](https://www.jetadmin.io/blog/supabase-pricing-2026-guide-to-plans-limits-and-real-world-costs/); [MakerKit](https://makerkit.dev/blog/saas/supabase-pricing)
- **Firebase [secondary].** Spark plan free; Blaze pay-as-you-go with a $300 credit for eligible new accounts and no spending cap by default; since 3 February 2026 Cloud Storage for Firebase needs a linked Blaze billing account even at light usage. — [DevToolLab](https://devtoollab.com/blog/firebase-alternatives); [Unico Connect](https://unicoconnect.com/blogs/backend-as-a-service-providers-compared)
- **Convex [secondary].** Professional about $25 per developer per month (priced per seat, not per project); Enterprise tier from $2,500/month added April 2026. — [DevToolPicks](https://devtoolpicks.com/blog/best-firebase-alternatives-indie-hackers-2026); [Cut the SaaS](https://cut-the-saas.com/alternatives/convex)
- **Cloudflare [GitHub, fetched].** Workers Free: 100,000 requests per day, 10 ms CPU per invocation. Workers Paid: $5/month per account including 10 million requests (then $0.30 per million) and 30 million CPU-ms (then $0.02 per million). R2: 10 GB-month free, then $0.015 per GB-month, egress free. — [Cloudflare docs source](https://github.com/cloudflare/cloudflare-docs/blob/production/src/content/docs/workers/platform/pricing.mdx)

**Subscription and paywall platforms (adds to first pass)**
- **RevenueCat [secondary].** Free to $2,500 monthly tracked revenue, then 1% of tracked revenue. — [PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/revenuecat-vs-adapty-vs-superwall-mobile-in-app-2026); [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/revenuecat-vs-superwall-vs-adapty-in-app-subscription-indie-2026)
- **Adapty [secondary; sources conflict].** Removed its $99/month minimum in February 2026; free tier to $5,000 monthly tracked revenue per its 2026 pricing, while older listings still say $10,000. — [App Pricing Lab review](https://apppricinglab.com/playbook/adapty-review); [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/revenuecat-vs-superwall-vs-adapty-in-app-subscription-indie-2026)
- **Superwall [secondary].** Subscription infrastructure free on every tier; the paywall product is billed at about 1% of paywall-attributed revenue; free ceiling not retrieved. — [PricePush](https://pricepush.app/blog/superwall-vs-revenuecat-numbers); [Newly guide](https://newly.app/guides/revenuecat-vs-superwall)

**Analytics, crash reporting, builds**
- **PostHog [vendor/secondary, consistent].** Free 1 million product-analytics events per month plus 5,000 web and 2,500 mobile session replays; no seat pricing; each product meters separately. — [PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/posthog-vs-mixpanel.md); [Usercall](https://usercall.co/post/posthog-pricing)
- **Mixpanel, Amplitude, TelemetryDeck [secondary; conflicting].** Mixpanel free tier reported as 1M events or 100K tracked users, paid from $20 or $100/month; Amplitude free tier reported as 10K or 50K tracked users; TelemetryDeck about $8/month from one comparison page. Not usable without vendor pages. — [AgentDeals comparison](https://agentdeals.dev/analytics-free-tier-comparison-2026); [CostBench](https://costbench.com/compare/posthog-vs-mixpanel/)
- **Sentry [vendor page via search].** Developer plan free: 5,000 errors/month, one user. Team from $26/month billed annually with 50,000 errors (a November 2026 tracker shows $29). — [Sentry pricing](https://sentry.io/pricing/?utm_category=ai); [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/developer-tools/sentry/free-plan/)
- **Expo EAS [GitHub, fetched].** Free plan: limited low-priority builds. Starter: "$19 per month, you get $45 of build credit". — [Expo docs source](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/main/docs/pages/billing/plans.mdx)

**Typical totals**
- **Blog estimates [secondary, unverified].** Tooling of $0-200/month for an indie iOS app; "most indie apps that reach $5K/month operate on under $300/month total infrastructure"; a whole stack at $0-50/month below 100 users. — [The Swift Kit](https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/how-to-monetize-ios-app-indie-developer); [You Could Ship This](https://youcouldshipthis.com/blog/indie-app-revenue-realistic-expectations); [DEV playbook](https://dev.to/truongpx396/the-solo-founder-playbook-zero-hero-3j7d)

### Inferences
- Budget model from the list prices above (my arithmetic; assumes Supabase + RevenueCat + PostHog + Sentry, no AI): **pre-traction** about $8/month (Apple fee only, all free tiers); **launched, about 1k monthly users** $8-35 (add Supabase Pro at $25 to avoid project pausing); **about 10k monthly users** roughly $60-100 fixed (Supabase Pro $25, Sentry Team $26, optional EAS $19, Apple $8) plus 1% of tracked revenue once above $2,500/month; **about 100k monthly users** the Supabase MAU quota is still inside the $25 plan, so the bill is driven by compute tier, egress beyond 250 GB, analytics events beyond 1M and the 1% revenue fee (for example $300 on $30k tracked revenue), most likely a few hundred dollars. The 100k figure is an estimate, not a reported bill.
- An on-device-first design (SwiftData/CloudKit private database, on-device model) removes the backend line entirely; CloudKit's cost to the developer is effectively zero at indie scale, at the price of Apple-only lock-in.
- Firebase's uncapped Blaze billing is the main bill-shock risk; Supabase and Cloudflare have predictable floors ($25 and $5).

### Gaps
- Real monthly bills at 1k, 10k and 100k users for any backend: none with stated workloads found (one "$97 busy month on Supabase Pro" figure could not be traced).
- Firebase unit prices (Firestore reads/writes, Auth, Functions): not retrieved. CloudKit numeric request and transfer quotas: not on the fetched page.
- Mixpanel, Amplitude and TelemetryDeck current free tiers; Firebase Crashlytics terms; Superwall free ceiling; EAS Production price: not confirmed.
- Web-billing costs (Stripe, Paddle) remain unverified from the first pass.

## 3. AI feature unit costs in 2026 (model prices, usage caps, margins, Apple's on-device and free server-side models)

### Takeaway
Small-model inference is now cheap enough that a text or vision request costs a fraction of a cent, so cost risk comes from free users and heavy users rather than from the price per call; and since WWDC 2026 Apple gives Small Business Program developers with fewer than 2 million first-time downloads server-side Apple Foundation Models at no API cost, with Apple-controlled per-user daily limits and a 6-month exit clause.

### Cited Findings

**Apple's models (primary)**
- **Free server-side tier [Apple, fetched].** "If you're enrolled in the App Store Small Business Program with fewer than two million first-time App Store downloads, you're eligible to use Apple Foundation Models running on Private Cloud Compute (PCC) with no cloud API cost." Requirements: Small Business Program, fewer than 2 million first-time downloads "from any of their apps", and the PCC entitlement. "If any app subsequently exceeds the 2 million first-time downloads threshold, or the developer is no longer enrolled ... the developer will be notified and must migrate to an alternative solution within 6 months." TestFlight installs do not count. — [Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/); [Accessing Private Cloud Compute](https://developer.apple.com/private-cloud-compute/)
- **Limits [Apple, fetched].** "Each user gets a daily limit. And users can upgrade to iCloud+ to get higher limits"; "Requests are counted with your user's iCloud account"; at the limit the request throws `quotaLimitReached`. No numeric limit is published: "Daily limits may vary by feature, request complexity, system demand, system policies, and other factors. Expanded access to such features will be available for a fee in the future." — [WWDC26 session 319](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/319/); [PCC documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/adding-server-side-intelligence-with-private-cloud-compute); [Apple news, 3 Nov 2026](https://developer.apple.com/news/)
- **Capabilities [Apple, fetched].** PCC model: 32K-token context, reasoning levels light/moderate/deep, no API keys or accounts, requires network, iOS 27 / macOS 27 / watchOS 27 / visionOS 27 or later and an Apple Intelligence device. On-device model: unlimited use, offline, context 4,096 tokens on OS 26.0 and 8,192 on 27.0 (newer devices), image input, guided generation into Swift types, tool calling. — [WWDC26 session 319](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/319/); [WWDC26 session 241](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/); [Foundation Models docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels)
- **Third-party models through the same API [Apple, fetched].** "You can now work with any language model, including Apple Foundation Models, cloud models like Claude and Gemini"; "Anthropic, and Google are both publishing Swift packages"; these are "billed per-token" and need the developer's own authentication. — [WWDC26 Apple Intelligence guide](https://developer.apple.com/wwdc26/guides/apple-intelligence/); [WWDC26 session 241](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/241/)
- **Credit packs [Apple, fetched].** Generative-AI apps can request an entitlement for expiring consumables that "must have a minimum expiration term of 12 months"; the condition quoted is that developers "must pay usage-based API costs for each request or output". — [App Store what's new](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/whats-new/)

- **Transcription [vendor page via search].** gpt-4o-transcribe $0.006 per minute; gpt-4o-mini-transcribe $0.003 per minute. — [OpenAI pricing](https://developers.openai.com/docs/pricing)

**Margins and caps**
- **Per-user cost range [vendor estimate; Inworld sells inference].** A conversational consumer app costs about $0.09 to $18.24 per daily active user per month at July 2026 list prices, depending on small-text versus flagship-voice stack; if about 3% of users pay and free users consume similarly, each payer covers about 33 users' usage. — [Inworld](https://inworld.ai/resources/unit-economics-of-consumer-ai-apps)
- **Gross margin benchmark [third-party report, relayed].** ICONIQ State of AI 2026: average AI product gross margin 45% in 2025, projected 53% in 2026, versus 60-80% for traditional software. — [1752vc](https://www.1752.vc/learn/ai-gross-margins-and-inference-costs)
- **Pricing heuristics [vendor blogs].** Price at 3.5-5x token cost (70-80% gross margin); set the base price on the median user's cost, model the 90th-percentile user and cap usage near 3x the median; models in use are prepaid credits, flat plans with caps, and usage-based markup. A worked example shows a power user driving a $200 seat to -180% margin. — [Paritydeals](https://www.paritydeals.com/ai-margin-calculator/); [Causo](https://hub.causo.ai/guides/how-to-price-ai-product-token-costs-margins-2026); [Ertas](https://www.ertas.ai/blog/ai-startup-gross-margins-inference)

### Inferences
- Unit-cost arithmetic from the list prices (my assumptions, not sourced: a photo-analysis request of about 1,500 input and 300 output tokens; image token counts vary by model and resolution): about $0.0003 on Claude Haiku 5.5, $0.0007 on GPT-5.4 nano, $0.0008 on Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, $0.0025 on GPT-5.4 mini, $0.006 on Claude Sonnet 5.5. A user making 150 such requests a month costs about $0.05-0.40 on small models and about $0.90 on a mid-tier model.
- Chat arithmetic (assumed 600 messages a month at 2,000 input and 300 output tokens): about $0.21 per user per month on Haiku 5.5, about $1.70 on GPT-5.4 mini. Image generation is the expensive modality: 100 images a month costs about $1.10 at gpt-image-1-mini medium, $3.60 at high, about $6.70 on Gemini 3.1 Flash Image. Sixty minutes of transcription costs about $0.18.
- Against a $10 monthly price ($8.50 after 15% commission), small-model text or vision features cost a paying user under 5% of net revenue. The margin problem is free usage: with a 33-to-1 free-to-paid ratio even $0.10 per free user consumes $3.30 per payer. Hence hard paywalls or tight free caps for AI features, and model routing (small model by default).
- Apple's free PCC tier can take the server bill to zero for an eligible indie app, but only on iOS 27+ Apple Intelligence devices, with a daily cap the developer does not control, and with a forced migration within 6 months of passing 2 million downloads. A sensible design keeps a paid-API fallback behind the same `LanguageModel` protocol.

- OpenAI GPT-5.6 standard prices and current Google Flash prices: conflicting or missing; verify on vendor pages.
- Per-image vision token counts by provider: not retrieved (the arithmetic above uses an assumption).

## 4. Retention and re-engagement evidence (benchmarks, push, widgets, Live Activities, streaks, onboarding, ratings, referrals, events, offers)

### Takeaway
Hard causal evidence exists almost only from Duolingo's streak experiments (single-digit to +14% relative retention lifts per change); widget and Live Activity figures are correlations between adopters and non-adopters. iOS push opt-in sits around 49-56%, and Apple shipped three new first-party retention tools in 2026 (cancel-flow retention messages, monthly billing with a 12-month commitment, peer benchmarks in Analytics).

- **Widgets [Google case study, Android].** Gratitude: 25% higher retention among widget users; 10% of daily actives adopted widgets; about 1,000 weekly journal entries from widgets. — [Android Developers Blog](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/gratitude-saw-25-higher-retention-for-widget-users)

**Onboarding length and quizzes**
- **Screen counts [teardowns].** Noom's web-to-app funnel: up to 113 screens, 10-15 minutes, paywall after about 100. - **Rating prompt [Apple, fetched].** "You can prompt for ratings up to three times in a 365-day period"; ask "when users are most likely to feel satisfaction ... such as when they've completed an action, level, or task". — [Ratings and reviews](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/ratings-and-reviews/)
- **In-app events [Apple, fetched].** Up to 10 published and 15 approved events; each up to 31 days, promotable 14 days ahead. In search, the event card replaces screenshots "for users who have downloaded your app"; users can opt in to a start notification. — [In-App Events](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/in-app-events/)
- **Retention messages [Apple, fetched; new 4 November 2026].** Messages "appear in the cancellation flow and can include text, images, or a special offer"; configured in App Store Connect; the Retention Messaging API (access by request) selects the message per subscriber. — [Apple developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/)
- **Monthly with 12-month commitment [Apple, fetched; 27 April 2026].** A monthly-paid plan with a one-year commitment, available worldwide except the United States and Singapore on OS 26.4+. — [Apple developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/)
- **Win-back and billing recovery [Apple, fetched].** Win-back offers show on the product page, in an automatic in-app sheet, in subscription settings and by direct link. Billing grace period 3, 16 or 28 days; Apple retries failed renewals for 60 days; up to 10 promotional offers per subscription. — [Subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- **Referrals [vendor heuristics, unsourced].** 5-25% of activated users send an invite and 15-40% of invites install; "above 0.3 is meaningful, above 1.0 is viral". No measured benchmark for subscription apps. — [SEM Nexus](https://semnexus.com/in-app-referral-mechanics-design-loop-that-compounds/); [Tenjin glossary](https://tenjin.com/glossary/k-factor/)
- **Small self-reported lever.** Habit Pixel's founder credits an in-app changelog shown on each update, with public credit to users, as the biggest driver on the way to $1K monthly recurring revenue. — [Indie Hackers, Jan 2026](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-0-to-1k-mrr-in-8-months-bootstrapping-habit-pixel-as-a-solo-dev-684b6c056d)

### Inferences
- The only experimentally measured mechanics are Duolingo's, and each change moved retention by a few percent relative (largest +14% at day 7). A small app should expect streaks, reminders and badges to compound in small steps, not to transform a weak core loop.
- Widget and Live Activity "uplifts" (23.7%, 25%) compare self-selected engaged users with the rest; they justify building the surface where the product has glanceable state, not a forecast of retention gain.
- About half of iOS users grant push permission, and a pre-permission explanation during onboarding is the cheapest lever on that rate.
- Long personalised onboarding is standard among top subscription apps (25-35 screens for apps, 100+ for web funnels), but the evidence supports "show a personalised result before the price", not length itself.
- Apple's 2026 additions move the save attempt to the moment of cancellation and add a commitment plan outside the US; both are free to adopt and should precede third-party win-back spending.

## 5. Pricing (price points by category, trends, localization, lifetime purchases, family plans, student discounts)

### Takeaway
The US reference points in 2026 are about $5.99 weekly, $10 monthly and $35 annually (RevenueCat medians), with annual prices rising and weekly plans taking over revenue share; lifetime and one-time purchases are growing as a share of revenue. No controlled public result was found for purchasing-power price localization, and most indie apps still do not do it.

### Cited Findings
- **Overall medians [vendor data, RevenueCat 2026 via search summary].** Median yearly price rose to $34.80 from $31.60; monthly $10 and weekly $5.99 were flat. — [RevenueCat report page](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)

Gaming: $24.99 yearly and $4.99 monthly on RevenueCat's page; the secondary table says $20.55 yearly (conflict). — [RevenueCat Education](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-education); [RevenueCat Gaming](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-gaming); [Airbridge table](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-app-pricing-by-category-2026-benchmark)

### Inferences
- A defensible US starting ladder for a consumer utility or lifestyle app is $5.99-6.99 weekly, $9.99 monthly, $34.99-39.99 annually, with a lifetime option at roughly 3-4x annual ($99-149) as an anchor; Education and Health & Fitness sustain $39.99-44.99 annually.

### Inferences
- Planning model (my synthesis; the timeline rows rest on one documented case and blog ranges): build 1-3 months with AI agents; first $1K monthly revenue 6-12 months after launch if the app is among those that get there at all (the first pass found most do not); $10K in 2-4 years for organic solo apps, faster only with a working paid or creator channel.
