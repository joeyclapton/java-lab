# Vertical App Store niches, second pass: professions, hobbies, health conditions and life admin (state as of 13 Nov 2026)

Method and reliability note (read before using any number): 35 web searches were run on 13 Nov 2026 and that was the hard budget. Page fetching was blocked for almost every host, so nearly all figures below were read from search-result summaries, not from the pages themselves. Two fetches were attempted: Apple's App Store Review Guidelines (worked, quoted verbatim in section 4) and the TrustMRR page for BritPass (failed, host not allowed).

Labels used in every table:
- **3P** = third-party modeled estimate (Adapty paywall library, Screensdesign, AppGoblin, Similarweb, ZoomInfo, Prospeo, Growjo, Latka). None is audited. Unless a date is given, the snapshot date is unknown and the figure is "as retrieved 13 Nov 2026".
- **SR** = self-reported by the company or founder (press, blog, investor page, store listing).
- **V** = revenue shown on a public listing as verified against a payment or subscription provider.
- **List** = store list price.

Estimator spread is very large. For the same app, AppGoblin (which shows banded floors such as "$10K+", "$50K+", "<$10K") and Adapty or Screensdesign (point estimates) differ by 5x to 50x: Flighty $10K+ vs $500K per month, Huckleberry $10K+ vs $2M, onX Hunt $50K+ vs $8M. Treat every 3P figure as order-of-magnitude only.

## 1. Exam and certification prep (driving theory, nursing, real estate, IT/PMP, citizenship)

### Takeaway
Deadline-driven test prep is the best-evidenced indie-friendly niche in this pass: one small citizenship-test app shows RevenueCat-verified revenue of $92K in 30 days, and the format (a question bank plus short paid passes of $3 to $125) repeats across driving, nursing, IT and trade exams. The big US exams are crowded with dozens of sub-$10K clones, so the opening is in specific exams, countries or languages that the portfolio publishers have not reached.

### Inferences
- The paywall pattern is a short pass sized to the study window, not a long subscription: $2.99 per week or $9.99 one-time (citizenship), $20.99 per month to $124.99 per year (professional certs), $35 to $75 for one to three months (real estate), $39 to $140 (NCLEX). Price tolerance rises with the stakes and cost of the exam.
- BritPass showing MRR $0 and 6 active subscriptions next to $92K in 30 days suggests most of its revenue is the one-time $9.99 pass, not the weekly plan. At list price that would be roughly 9,000 purchases a month; I could not verify the size of the UK test-taker population, so the plausibility of that figure is unchecked. Storefront prices vary, which may explain part of it.
- The winning structure is a portfolio on one engine: Pocket Prep (14+ apps), Driving Test Success and Zutobi all reuse a quiz engine across many question banks. A solo developer can copy the structure but needs a content pipeline, not more code.
- Crowding is exam-specific. US real estate (17+ clones under $10K) and NCLEX (strong web-first incumbents such as UWorld and Archer) are poor entry points. A verified $92K month from an app whose copyright line starts in 2025 shows that a newer entrant can still win a single national test.
- Built-in churn is the structural weakness: every customer leaves after passing, so revenue depends on continuous acquisition (App Store search and paid search) rather than retention. That also means renewal-rate benchmarks are the wrong yardstick here; revenue per install is the right one.
- Generic "AI study" tools ($115 to $134 MRR in the two verified listings) earn far less than exam-specific products, which suggests the value is in the exam-specific question bank and pass guarantee, not the AI layer.

### Gaps
- No revenue data was found for PMP, CompTIA or language-certificate (IELTS, TOEFL, DELE, Goethe) apps specifically, for DMV Genie, or for US citizenship (USCIS civics) apps. Not searched: driving-theory apps outside the UK and US (e.g. Germany, France, Brazil).
- The BritPass figures come from a search snippet of a TrustMRR page whose layout is known to mix numbers from neighbouring listings; the direct fetch failed (trustmrr.com is outside the session's allowed domains). The founder was not identified.
- No source was found on question-bank licensing costs or copyright constraints. Unverified background knowledge: some official bodies license their revision question banks to publishers; this needs checking per exam before building.
- No data on refund rates or on paid acquisition cost for exam keywords.

### Inferences
- Invoice and estimate apps behave like small B2B subscriptions: Saldo's Invoice Maker is estimated at about $70K per month on under 1K new downloads a month, which only makes sense if an installed base keeps renewing. That matches the first-pass finding that Business apps have the joint-highest median annual renewal (40%) but the slowest ramp to $1K MRR (113 days) — [RevenueCat 2026, via first-pass notes](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/).
- Weekly pricing of $9.99 to $11.99 on a business tool (Saldo) is about five times the monthly price of Invoice Home ($6.99). Trades users accept high effective prices when the app gets them paid.
- The generic "invoice maker" keyword is saturated (20+ listings, most under $10K). The realistic entry for a small team is a trade-specific or country-specific version (tax format, local payment rails, trade price lists) rather than another generic maker. This is a reasoned guess; no evidence on vertical-specific invoice apps was collected.
- Mileage tracking sells a tax deduction, so the return is easy to explain; MileIQ raised its price 50% in 2025 and is still estimated near $1M per month. The counterweight is Stride, which is free because it monetizes through insurance referrals.
- Aviation, clinical reference, trucking and salon software all show professional price tolerance, but each sits on a moat a solo developer cannot build quickly: certified aeronautical data, licensed drug content, a crowd-sourced driver network, or payments and booking marketplaces.
- Guideline 3.1.3(d) lets a tool for trainers, tutors or agents take one-to-one service payments outside in-app purchase, which protects margin for a micro-CRM that handles client payments.

### Gaps
- No revenue found for Invoice Simple, Invoice2go, Jobber, Everlance, Stride, Gridwise, Circuit, Spoke, Zeo, ForeFlight (revenue or current list prices), MDCalc, Trainerize or Everfit.
- Not searched: real-estate agent tools, hair and beauty tools for solo professionals below GlossGenius scale, construction estimators with AI take-off, delivery-driver earnings trackers beyond Gridwise.
- The "$3M revenue, profitable since June" statement about Trucker Path came from an undated article and is left out of the table.
- Unverified background knowledge: Joist and Invoice Simple are believed to belong to a larger field-service software group; ownership was not checked this session.

### Inferences
- The investor-stated 3 million onX subscribers at a minimum of $34.99 per year implies at least about $105M per year, close to Adapty's $8M per month and far above AppGoblin's "$50K+" and Similarweb's $15M to $25M. For large subscription apps the point estimators look nearer the truth than the banded ones; this calibration matters for every other row in these notes. The word "subscribers" may include free members, so this is not proof.
- What pays in hobbies is proprietary or hard-to-assemble data tied to a physical activity: land-ownership maps (onX), course maps and shot data (Golfshot, 18Birdies), resort and lift data (Slopes), forecasts (OpenSnow, Surfline). Pure logging and counting (row counters) does not.
- Seasonality cuts both ways: Slopes chose subscriptions because of the ski season, and OpenSnow reports very high annual renewal, so users who return each season behave like loyal annual payers; but cash arrives in a few months of the year.
- Racquet sports are being consolidated as venture-funded booking and rating networks (Playtomic with $136M raised, DUPR, SwingVision). A solo developer is better placed building a utility on top of those networks (scoring, video tagging, club ladders) than competing for the network itself. No revenue evidence was found for such utilities.
- Time to payoff is long in this category: Slopes took nine years to reach $1M ARR and earned about $10K in its first two years.
- Group or family plans appear to fit hobby apps: 42% of OpenSnow subscribers chose the Group plan.

- Not searched for lack of budget: birding, gardening and plant care, wine, coffee and whisky loggers, board-game and tabletop tools, running and cycling niche tools, tide and solunar apps specifically, swing-analysis apps, padel or pickleball scoring apps.

- Data-licensing costs (golf course maps, parcel data, marine charts, weather models) were not researched and are probably the main cost line for a new entrant.
- The Slopes revenue after 2022 could not be read; one tracker's "under $5K" figure contradicts the founder's own numbers and was discarded.

Snoring recording is the strongest single-symptom niche found (one 2012-vintage app estimated at $100K to $400K per month), with migraine tracking and app-delivered IBS therapy in a middle tier; blood-pressure logs, tinnitus sound apps and AI blood-test readers are crowded and mostly estimated under $10K per month. ### Inferences
- The health apps that earn are those that capture a signal the user cannot get otherwise and replay it nightly or per episode: SnoreLab records snoring with the microphone and sells $39.99 a year. Manual-entry logs for values measured elsewhere (blood pressure, tinnitus sound therapy) have many clones and little revenue.
- Program-style products (Nerva at $78.99 for three months) charge more per user than trackers and can add a payer channel, but they need clinical content and evidence, which is a bigger lift than an app.
- For a small team the safe zone is logging, pattern display, export for the doctor, and recording, with wellness wording. Diagnosis, treatment recommendations, dose calculation and sensor-only measurement claims run into guideline 1.4.1 or 1.4.2 or medical-device law. mySugr's 18-month certification is a guide to the cost of crossing that line.
- The January 2026 FDA revision mainly helps wearable makers. It could indirectly help apps that present data from those wearables, as one law firm suggests, but that is an interpretation, not settled guidance.
- AI blood-test readers face free substitutes from the companies that sell the tests and from general chat assistants, so stand-alone subscriptions look weak; every revenue estimate found was under $10K.
- Guideline 5.1.3 rules out an advertising or data-resale model for health trackers, which leaves subscriptions or one-time purchase as the only viable models.

### Gaps
- No revenue found for mySugr, Gluroo, Sugarmate or any CGM companion; a search summary called Sugarmate an "Authorized Dexcom Data Partner" without an identifiable source.
- Not searched: fertility, ADHD medication trackers, sleep-apnea screening apps, physiotherapy and posture apps, longevity memberships, Monash FODMAP or other low-FODMAP diet apps.
- EU MDR rules for health software, and current FDA status of specific apps, were not researched. Unverified background knowledge: a fertility-tracking app is believed to hold FDA clearance as a contraceptive, which would show the regulated route can be commercial.
- Whether Apple's own snoring, sleep-apnea or hypertension features reduce demand for third-party apps was not researched.

## 6. Ranked shortlist: the niches with the best evidence for a solo developer or small studio

### Takeaway
Ranking by quality of evidence and fit for a small team, the top of the list is deadline exam prep for a specific test, trade-specific invoicing and estimating, and data-backed passion-sport utilities; mileage tracking, snoring recording, flight tracking, baby sleep and vehicle-owner companions follow with good demand evidence but stronger incumbents. Only two revenue figures in this whole pass are better than a modeled estimate (BritPass, verified; Slopes, founder-reported), so the ranking is a judgement call built on order-of-magnitude numbers.

Key evidence behind each shortlisted niche (full rows and caveats are in sections 1 to 5):

### Inferences
The ranking below is this researcher's judgement. "Evidence" grades the data, not the opportunity: A = verified or founder-reported revenue for a small team; B = several third-party estimates that agree on order of magnitude; C = estimates that conflict by 10x or more.

| Rank | Niche | Why it suits a small team | Main risk | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deadline exam prep for one specific test (citizenship, driving theory, a licence or trade cert), ideally in a country or language the portfolios skip | Buyers have a date and a fee at stake, so they pay $10 to $125 without a trial; one quiz engine is reusable across many tests; search intent is explicit, so App Store search does the selling; no ads needed | Customers leave after passing, so revenue needs constant acquisition; big US exams already have 17+ clones; question content must be accurate, current and legally usable | A (one verified app) plus B |
| 2 | Trade-specific estimates and invoices on the phone | Business payers with high renewal; $7 to $32 per month, and weekly plans near $10 are accepted; the app is tied to getting paid, so it is hard to drop; low ad competition from consumer apps | Generic "invoice maker" keyword is saturated; slow ramp typical of Business apps (median 113 days to $1K MRR); incumbents bundle payments and accounting sync | B |
| 3 | Data-backed utility for one passion sport (snow, golf, fishing, hunting, stargazing, tides) | Enthusiasts renew annually and buy group plans; Apple features this kind of app (see first-pass notes on Tide Guide and Moonlitt); a solo developer has already taken one to $1M ARR | Slow build (nine years for Slopes); seasonal cash flow; map, course or forecast data may need licences; leaders are entrenched in the biggest sports | A (Slopes) plus B/C |
| 4 | Mileage and earnings tracking for self-employed drivers | The app pays for itself through tax deductions; the leader raised price 50% in 2025; opening for country-specific tax rules outside the US | A free rival funded by insurance referrals; background location and battery complaints; tax rules differ by country, so each market is a separate product | B/C (one estimator for the leader) |
| 5 | Recording-based single-symptom health tools (snoring first; migraine diaries second) | Captures a signal the user cannot see otherwise; nightly use builds habit; $40 to $100 per year; one small UK publisher has led since 2012 | Wording must stay on the wellness side of guideline 1.4.1 and medical-device rules; Apple and wearables keep adding native sleep and health features; ads and data resale are barred by 5.1.3 | B (snoring), C (migraine) |
| 6 | Premium flight and trip tracking for frequent travelers | High price tolerance ($59.99 per year, $299 lifetime); Travel has the joint-highest annual renewal in RevenueCat's 2026 data (first-pass notes) | Flight-data feeds cost money per user; a polished incumbent; operating-system features increasingly show flight status natively | C |
| 7 | Baby sleep and feeding tracking | New cohort of parents every year; sleep problems create urgency; about $59 per year accepted | One dominant app; each customer ages out within two to three years; advice on infant sleep carries responsibility | C |
| 8 | Companion apps for owners of one vehicle brand or device | Proven by a tiny unfunded team; owner communities spread the word; subscription tied to an expensive asset | Total dependence on the maker's API terms and pricing; a single brand caps the market | C |

Niches examined and left off the list, with the reason:
- Travel eSIM: very large and growing, but a telecom-reseller business with venture-funded leaders.
- Aviation, clinical reference, trucking networks, salon booking: strong professional price tolerance, but data-licence or network moats held by scaled companies.
- Blood-pressure logs, tinnitus sound apps, AI blood-test readers, pet-health trackers, knitting row counters, US real-estate exam prep: many clones and nearly every estimate under $10K per month.
- AAC: highest unit prices and institutional buyers, but no revenue data and a high bar for clinical credibility; worth a dedicated follow-up rather than a ranking.
- Racquet-sport booking and rating networks: being consolidated by funded companies; utilities built on top of them are unproven.

Cross-cutting observations:
- Revenue follows a deadline, a deduction or a data layer. Exam dates, tax returns and ski seasons give people a reason to pay now; pure logging does not.
- Short, high-priced passes (weekly or one-off) work where the need is temporary; annual plans work where the activity recurs each season.
- The trackers disagree most on large subscription apps, and the one cross-check available (onX subscribers times list price) sided with the higher point estimates. Banded "under $10K" labels on long-tail apps are probably directionally right, since they agree across dozens of clones.

### Gaps
- Only two figures in this pass rest on something better than a model: BritPass (verified listing, read from a snippet) and Slopes (founder-reported, 2022). A paid data source (Appfigures, Sensor Tower or AppMagic) is needed before committing to any niche.
- No keyword-difficulty, cost-per-install, trial-conversion or churn data was collected for any shortlisted niche, so "low ad competition" claims are untested.
- Whole areas named in the brief remain unresearched after two passes: birding, gardening, wine and coffee loggers, board-game tools, running and cycling tools, fertility, ADHD medication, physiotherapy, itinerary and packing, EV charging and OBD, home maintenance, subscription and warranty trackers, caregiver apps, kids education, real-estate agent tools, language-certificate prep.
- Non-English markets were not examined in this pass; whether rank 1 and rank 2 transfer to markets such as Brazil should be checked against the separate Brazil and Latin America notes.
