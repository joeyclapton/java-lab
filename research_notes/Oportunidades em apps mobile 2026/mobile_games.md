## 1. Market size and trend 2024-2026 (IAP, ads, downloads, iOS vs Android, genres)

### Takeaway
Mobile games are a very large but flat-to-shrinking market: about $82B of IAP in 2025 (+1%), downloads down about 7%, and H1 2026 IAP down 2% with downloads down 12%; in 2025 non-game apps out-earned games on mobile for the first time. Revenue is concentrated in strategy/4X, puzzle, RPG and casino at the top, with hybrid-casual the only clearly growing "small-scope" segment, and iOS takes roughly 64% of IAP.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 full year (Sensor Tower, State of Gaming 2026, published Feb 2026): mobile game IAP revenue about $82B, up about 1.4% YoY (one summary says +1%); mobile game downloads about 50.4B, down about 7.2% YoY; about 52B game downloads across all platforms. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/over-95000-mobile-games-were-downloaded-every-minute-in-2025); [Global Games Forum](https://www.globalgamesforum.com/news/state-of-gaming-heres-what-the-mobile-data-actually-says); [Sensor Tower press release](https://sensortower.com/press/press-release-sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-gaming-drove-52-billion-downloads-82b-iap-revenue-on-mobile-and-12b-premium-revenue-on-steam); [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)
- Sensor Tower puts total 2025 gaming revenue it tracks at $94B = about $82B mobile IAP + about $12B premium revenue on Steam. — [Sensor Tower press release via Bolsamania](https://www.bolsamania.com/nota-de-prensa/mercados/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-gaming-drove-94-billion-in-revenue-in-2025-downloads-reached-52-billion--21828650.html)
- iOS vs Android, 2025: App Store game revenue about $52.5B (+0.6% YoY) vs about $30B on Google Play (Sensor Tower figures, surfaced in a search summary attributed to 9to5Mac, Feb 2026; the article URL was not captured, so verify). The two add up to the $82B total, i.e. iOS is about 64% of IAP. — [Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-gaming-2026) (verify)
- In 2025, mobile games earned less consumer spend than non-game apps for the first time (Sensor Tower State of Mobile 2026, Jan 2026). — [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-the-state-of-the-mobile); [GamesBeat headline "Apps beat games in mobile spend for the first time in a decade"](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=315027)
- H1 2026 (Sensor Tower): mobile game IAP fell 2% YoY to $40B; downloads fell 12% to 24B. — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/mobile-gaming-iap-revenue-drops-2-as-ad-spend-surges-in-2026); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-gaming-iap-dipped-2-as-ad-spend-climbed-in-h1-2026/)
- H1 2026 IAP by genre (Sensor Tower): strategy $9.3B, puzzle $8.1B, RPG $6.1B, casino $5.0B. Share of genre ad spend (UA spend): puzzle 33%, strategy 21%, RPG 9.6%, casino 9.2%. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-gaming-iap-dipped-2-as-ad-spend-climbed-in-h1-2026/)

- 2025 regional genre deltas (Sensor Tower, via an unofficial mirror of the report, so verify): strategy +$1.38B in Asia and +$1.12B in North America; RPG -$1.53B in Asia; casino -$860M in North America; puzzle +$706M in Europe. — [report mirror](https://sensortower-2026-gaming.pages.dev/)
- Puzzle IAP was about $8.7B in 2025 per AppMagic. — [MobileGamer.biz data digest, Jan 2026](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-2025s-top-earning-genres-appsflyer-for-sale-block-blast-hits-70m-dau-app-store-earnings-more/)

- Hybrid-casual, 2025: Sensor Tower says it is the only segment with meaningful IAP growth, +20% YoY to $4.2B; AppMagic-based reporting gives a far narrower definition, $788M (+75.1% YoY) with downloads of 14.1B (-4.1%), puzzle the main driver. — [Game World Observer, Jan 21 2026](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/01/21/in-2025-the-revenue-of-the-mobile-gaming-market-grew-but-only-slightly-analysis); [AppMagic Mobile Market Landscape 2026 (GameDev Reports summary)](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-market-landscape) (verify)

A commentator notes AppMagic's segment definition blurs hypercasual and hybrid-casual, so part of the "growth" is reclassification. — [AppMagic Monetization Report 2025 (PDF)](https://appmagic.rocks/files/img-blog/EN_Monetization_Report_2025.pdf); [GameDev Reports on AppMagic live ops 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-live-ops-in-2025)

### Inferences
- 135 games at $50.8B is about 62% of the $82B IAP total, so the IAP economy is a winner-take-most market run by large live-ops studios. A 1-3 person team is not competing for that pool; the realistic pools are advertising (about $12B by Sensor Tower's count), small premium, and niche subscription.
- Falling downloads (-7% in 2025, -12% in H1 2026) with flat IAP means fewer installs are worth more each, which pushes CPI up and penalises games that depend on cheap volume, i.e. the classic hypercasual model.
- iOS is about 64% of IAP but a minority of downloads, so iOS-first makes sense for IAP/premium/subscription, while ad-monetised volume plays lean on Android reach. For a Brazil-based developer the home market is Android-heavy and low-eCPM, so an iOS game aimed at US/EU players is a different business from a Portuguese-language Android game.
- The two ad-revenue estimates ($12B vs an implied $26B+) are not reconcilable; quote Sensor Tower's as the conservative floor and say the true number is disputed.

### Gaps
- No full-year 2025 revenue table by genre and no iOS-vs-Android split by genre (in the Sensor Tower report, not reachable this session).
- No 2024 baseline numbers were pulled directly (only YoY deltas), and no full-year 2026 forecast.
- Simulation, casino and idle revenue levels and trends beyond the points above; downloads by country (US vs Brazil vs India).
- The App Store vs Google Play 2025 split needs its original article URL.

## 2. Genre taxonomy by feasibility for small teams

### Takeaway
The segments where the money is growing (strategy/4X, match-3, RPG, casino, hybrid-casual at scale) are run by large, data-heavy studios; what a 1-3 person team can physically build are block/sort/word/daily puzzles, card and solitaire, idle, small premium games and niche narrative or party games, where the product is cheap but distribution is the hard part. Sourced per-genre benchmarks for cost, retention and LTV are thin, so the table below is analyst judgment anchored on a few hard numbers.

### Cited Findings
- Hybrid-casual is the growth segment: +20% IAP to $4.2B in 2025 (Sensor Tower definition); top hybrid-casual titles have overtaken top casual titles on D7 retention; the top 1,000 hybrid-casual games earn most of their revenue from IAP in major markets, with Brazil a notable exception (ads still dominate there). — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/01/21/in-2025-the-revenue-of-the-mobile-gaming-market-grew-but-only-slightly-analysis); [GameDev Reports, Sensor Tower State of Gaming 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026?open=false)
- Hybrid-casual revenue mix varies widely by sub-type in vendor guides (2026): IAP 40-60% of revenue blended; about 45/55 IAP/ads for puzzle and merge hybrids; about 82/18 IAP/ads for action and strategy hybrids; another guide says IAP is 59-82% depending on class. These are vendor heuristics, not measured panels. — [Game Growth Advisor, Mar 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/); [Game Growth Advisor, Apr 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/); [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-genres-list)
- Hybrid-casual CPI was $0.95 in 2024, about double the year before, while the all-games average CPI fell to $0.36 (Adjust, Mobile Games Insights 2025). — [GameDev Reports on Adjust](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-mobile-games-insights-report)
- Hybrid-casual puzzle (e.g. Color Block Jam) was the driver of the segment in Q1 2025. — [Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/hybrid-casual-puzzle-games-q1-2025/)
- Block puzzle at the top is an ad business at giant scale: Block Blast reports 70M DAU / 300M MAU (Dec 2025, company figure), with third-party estimates of roughly $584K-$1M per day in ad revenue and negligible IAP. Hungry Studio says it ran 10,000+ experiments in a year, so this is not a small-team operation. — [Business Wire, Jan 8 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260108572322/en); [Udonis (AppMagic and Gamigion estimates)](https://www.blog.udonis.co/statistics/block-blast)
- Puzzle is the most expensive genre to market: 33% of all game UA spend in H1 2026 goes to puzzle. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-gaming-iap-dipped-2-as-ad-spend-climbed-in-h1-2026/)
- Word/daily puzzle: NYT Games puzzles were played 11.2B times in 2025, Wordle alone 4.2B; the NYT bought Wordle (built by one person) in early 2022 for a price "in the low seven figures"; in Q1 2025 Games added about 110K subscribers, more than News (81K); the NYT launched its first two-player game in January 2026. — [AP via WTOP, Jan 21 2026](https://wtop.com/consumer-news/2026/01/this-just-in-from-the-new-york-times-its-first-2-player-game-and-an-evolving-business-model/); [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/news-game-bundle-why-new-york-times-q1-2025-performance-win-wall-street-1733651); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/new-york-times-earnings-wordle-acquisition-users-0cb11458-5b27-493b-a14b-d0d96eb82f7a.html)
- A publisher-backed word game from a small studio exists as a model: Kwalee publishes Story Giant Games' "Text Express: Word Adventure". — [Global Games Forum](https://globalgamesforum.com/kwalee-story-giant-games-unite-on-casual-title)
- Premium paid: Balatro (premium, $9.99) made about $1M in its first week on mobile and about $4.4M gross in two months (AppMagic), and later reporting puts it at 3.1M mobile downloads and $21.3M mobile revenue; AppMagic's own blog says "over $20M" lifetime. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/balatro-nears-44m-on-mobile-amid-a-sudden-spending-surge); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-46323) (verify for the $21.3M figure); [AppMagic blog](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/pc-games-to-mobile)
- Premium is described as "on the rise" on mobile, with Balatro a driving force. — [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/premium-mobile-games-increase)
- Small premium catalogue model: Tinytouchtales (Berlin, 1-3 people: one Unity developer plus art and sound support) reaches about 1.1M downloads a year across its card games. — [Unity case study](https://unity.com/case-study/tinytouchtales)
- Apple Arcade: funding upfront plus a "bonus pool" paid on "qualifying sessions"; developers report shrinking upfront fees and bonus payouts, cancelled projects, and a slow pitch process (2024 reporting; see section 7). — [MobileGamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/inside-apple-arcade-axed-games-declining-payouts-disillusioned-studios-and-an-uncertain-future/)
- Games inside other apps: Apple's Mini Apps Partner Program (Nov 13 2025) cuts commission to 15% on qualifying IAP in HTML5/JavaScript mini apps and mini games hosted inside a native app. — [Apple Developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=xcz1s7cz)
- Strategy/4X, RPG and casino are the top IAP genres ($9.3B, $6.1B, $5.0B in H1 2026) and carry the highest CPIs (casino iOS CPI $21-24, RPG iOS up to $36.5; see section 3). — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-gaming-iap-dipped-2-as-ad-spend-climbed-in-h1-2026/); [Liftoff 2025 Casual Gaming Apps Report](https://liftoff.ai/?p=35032)
- Retention reality across all genres (GameAnalytics): median D1 about 22%, top 25% just above 30%, top 10% about 40%; median D7 about 4%, top 10% about 12%; 75% of games have D28 below 3%; D1/D7/D30 fell again in 2025. — [GameAnalytics 2025 benchmarks (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-GameAnalytics-Mobile-Gaming-Benchmarks.pdf); [GameDev Reports on the 2026 edition](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)

### Inferences

## 3. Unit economics: CPI, eCPM, ARPDAU, test thresholds, payback

### Takeaway
Tier-1 iOS installs for casual genres cost several dollars (US puzzle about $9.5 on iOS in the latest AppsFlyer table) while ad-monetised casual games earn cents per daily user, so the median game cannot pay back paid UA; Brazil/LATAM installs cost $0.14-$0.43 but ad eCPMs there are a fraction of US levels. Publishers therefore gate prototypes on CPI under roughly $0.30-0.50 with D1 over 35% for hypercasual, or CPI under $1 plus deep playtime for hybrid-casual.

### Cited Findings
- AppsFlyer gaming benchmarks (table labelled Q3 2026): US puzzle CPI about $9.48 iOS / $4.81 Android; US casino about $24.45 iOS / $12.68 Android; Brazil hypercasual about $0.43 iOS / $0.14 Android; Brazil action about $1.33 iOS / $0.33 Android. — [AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/?p=549948)
- Adjust (2025 data): global blended game CPI $0.56; Latin America as low as $0.14; slots as high as $4.47; US median of $4.63 for Apple Ads search. — [GAMES.GG citing Adjust](https://games.gg/news/mobile-game-marketing-costs-2027/)
- Adjust (2024 data): hybrid-casual CPI $0.95; all-games average $0.36. — [GameDev Reports on Adjust](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-mobile-games-insights-report)
- Singular (Q2 2025): highest Android CPIs by country are US $3.25, China $2.30, Japan $2.05; on iOS, RPG is the most expensive genre at $36.50. — [GameDev Reports weekly recap, Jul 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/weekly-gaming-reports-recap-july-8d7?open=false) (verify)
- Liftoff 2025 Casual Gaming Apps Report (Feb 2024-Feb 2025): casino has the highest iOS CPI at $21.03; RPG the highest Android CPI at $4.29; simulation, action and racing cost about 10x more on iOS than Android. — [Liftoff](https://liftoff.ai/?p=35032)
- Ad eCPM, US (Appodeal, Q4 2024): rewarded video about $19.63 on iOS and $16.49 on Android. — [Appodeal Q4 2024 eCPM report (PDF)](https://appodeal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Quarterly_Mobile_eCPM_Report_-_Q4_2024.pdf); [Playio summary](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)
- eCPM trend (TopOn, H1 2025, global casual games): iOS rewarded video eCPM +18% YoY; Android rewarded -7% and Android interstitial -11%. — [TopOn H1 2025 report (PDF)](https://mores.toponad.com/reports/TopOn%20Global%20Mobile%20Games%20Monetization%20Report%20_%202025%20H1.pdf)
- eCPM trend (Q1-Q2 2026): iOS eCPMs rose at least 8% across formats in the US, UK, Germany, India and Japan, while Android rewarded video in India fell 5.18%. — [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)
- Emerging markets (Brazil, India, Philippines) have much lower eCPMs than the US, Japan and Australia; no exact 2025-2026 Brazil figure was found. — [MAF](https://maf.ad/en/blog/mobile-ads-ecpm/)
- ARPDAU, hybrid-casual (vendor heuristics, 2026): $0.15-$0.50 blended; $0.15-$0.25 "scalable", $0.35+ "elite". — [Game Growth Advisor, Mar 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-mobile-game-kpis-benchmarks-2026/); [Game Growth Advisor, Apr 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-04-16-hybrid-casual-game-design-strategy-2026/)
- Retention (Adjust): all-genre D1 of 27% in 2025, with hypercasual and hybrid-casual highest. — [GameDev Reports on Adjust 2026](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report)
- Retention (GameAnalytics): median D1 about 22%, top quartile just above 30%, top decile about 40%; median D7 about 4%, top decile about 12%; 75% of games under 3% at D28. — [GameAnalytics 2025 benchmarks (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-GameAnalytics-Mobile-Gaming-Benchmarks.pdf)
- Publisher test thresholds, Homa: hypercasual needs CPI under $0.40, D1 over 35%, D3 over 15%; hybrid-casual needs CPI under $1.00, 5,000 seconds of cumulative playtime by day 3, and 20% game completion; near-misses may get iteration. — [Homa knowledge base](https://support.homagames.com/knowledge/how-to-read-the-results-of-a-test); [Homa prototyping guide](https://support.homagames.com/knowledge/prototyping-for-hyper-casual)
- Publisher test thresholds, Supersonic: D1 of 38%+ on Android and about 15 minutes of playtime as the "sweet spot". — [Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/learn/blog/the-3-most-important-kpis-for-testing-your-hyper-casual-prototype/)
- Publisher test thresholds, Voodoo (older article, around 2020): lowered its bar to 45% D1, 13% D7 and $0.20 CPI. — [GameAnalytics blog](https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/how-voodoo-diversified-and-lowered-game-product-kpis)
- Other rules of thumb: $0.50 CPI, 35% D1, 8% D7 after the first CPI test; a kill-gate example of CPI under $0.30 and D1 over 35% within 72 hours. — [MAF](https://maf.ad/en/blog/tips-hyper-casual-prototypes/); [Bruin](https://getbruin.com/use-cases/mobile-gaming/hypercasual-soft-launch-kill-decision-framework/)
- Real ad-only yield at scale: Block Blast's estimated ad revenue is about $584K per day (Udonis/AppMagic) to $600K-$1M per day (Gamigion) against 17.3M DAU (AppMagic estimate) or 70M DAU (company figure). — [Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/statistics/block-blast); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260108572322/en)

### Inferences
- Block Blast's implied ARPDAU is about $0.034 on AppMagic's DAU ($584K / 17.3M) and about $0.009-$0.014 on the company's DAU ($600K-$1M / 70M). So a globally distributed, ad-only block puzzle earns roughly 1-3 cents per daily user, an order of magnitude below the $0.15-$0.50 vendor range for hybrid-casual.
- At $0.01-$0.03 ARPDAU, $5,000 a month ($167 a day) needs roughly 5,600-16,700 daily players sustained; $10,000 a month needs about 11,000-33,000. With median D7 at 4% and D28 under 3%, holding that DAU means a continuous inflow of installs.
- Illustrative payback (my arithmetic, assumptions stated): a median-retention game (D1 22%, D7 4%) yields maybe 2-3 active days per install; at $0.10 ARPDAU in the US that is about $0.20-$0.30 of lifetime value against a US iOS puzzle CPI of about $9.48. Even at top-decile retention the gap is an order of magnitude, so paid UA in tier-1 only works with IAP depth, exceptional creatives that pull CPI far below market, or organic/viral installs.
- Brazil arithmetic cuts both ways: CPI of $0.14-$0.43 is 20-60x cheaper than US puzzle, but eCPMs are also a fraction of US levels, so cheap Brazilian installs are useful for testing retention, not for profit.
- Publisher thresholds (CPI under $0.40) are measured on test campaigns of a short gameplay video and are not comparable with blended market CPIs; they are a test of "marketability", i.e. whether the concept's video gets clicked.
- iOS ad yields are rising while Android's fall (TopOn, Playio), which slightly favours iOS for ad-monetised games in tier-1 countries.

### Gaps
- No sourced 2025-2026 eCPM by format for Brazil, Mexico, India or tier-3 countries; no banner eCPMs; no interstitial eCPM table. (One summary mentioned US interstitial at about $11 Android vs $9.50 iOS without a resolvable source.)
- No sourced LTV by genre, no ROAS payback windows (D7/D30/D90 targets), no ARPDAU for casual, word, idle or solitaire.
- CPI by genre for Brazil beyond hypercasual and action; CPI for tier-3 countries.

## 4. The publisher model (hypercasual / hybrid-casual publishers and indie studios)

### Takeaway
Publishers still run open prototype-testing funnels with public KPI gates, but the pay-per-prototype era is fading in favour of revenue share, and no publisher publishes a hit rate. I found no Brazil- or LATAM-specific publisher programme.

### Cited Findings
- How the funnel works: the studio submits a prototype (usually a gameplay video plus a build), the publisher runs a paid CPI test and reads CPI, D1, playtime; games that pass get iteration support, then scaling. Homa's published gates are CPI under $0.40 / D1 over 35% / D3 over 15% for hypercasual and CPI under $1.00 / 5,000s playtime by D3 / 20% completion for hybrid-casual. — [Homa knowledge base](https://support.homagames.com/knowledge/how-to-read-the-results-of-a-test); [ironSource/Unity guide to marketability tests](https://is.com/community/academy/hyper-casual/how-to-run-marketability-tests-for-your-prototypes)
- Payments: Supersonic's "Super iteration package" page advertises up to $500 per prototype for a studio's next game and up to $2,000 a month while iterating on a current game (marketing page, may be out of date). — [Supersonic](https://supersonic.com/iteration-package)
- Pay-per-prototype is in decline; vendors describe publishers and studios moving away from it toward revenue share. — [MAF](https://maf.ad/en/blog/game-publishing-challenges/); [Tenjin](https://tenjin.com/?p=8302) (verify which carries the statement)
- Revenue share benchmarks (Voyer Law, publishing agreements in 2025, all game publishing, not hypercasual-specific): with no advance the developer keeps 67.9% on average (median 70%); with an advance, 58.2% on average. — [GameDev Reports on Voyer Law](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/voyer-law-publishing-agreements-in)
- Hypercasual anecdote (r/gamedev, Nov 2022): the split is on profit after UA spend, not gross; 50:50 is common and 60-70% to the developer is achievable. — [Reddit mirror](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/gamedev/comments/yxpejq/anyone_here_have_experience_dealing_with_hyper)
- Azur Games argues CPI is "not that important" and that it looks at other metrics; CrazyLabs markets "transparency" to studios; Kwalee runs a self-serve Publishing Portal where developers submit, market-test and get published. — [Azur Games](https://azurgames.com/blog/why-cpi-is-not-that-important-for-hyper-casual-or-what-metrics-were-looking-at); [PocketGamer.biz on CrazyLabs](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/comment-and-opinion/80564/a-deep-dive-into-crazylabs-transparency/); [Kwalee](https://www.kwalee.com/blog/welcome-kwalee-family-tictales)
- Publishers absorb the studios that work: Voodoo acquired Tarboosh Games after partnering with it, and signed Electric Manta for hybrid-casual games. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/voodoo-acquires-tarboosh-games-after-partnering-on-past-projects); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/electric-manta-partners-with-voodoo-to-bring-hybrid-casual-games)
- Instability on the publisher side: a PocketGamer.biz headline reports Unity will shut down the ironSource Ads network and explore a sale of Supersonic (date not captured; verify). — [PocketGamer.biz publishing tag](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/tags/publishing)
- A ranking of the top 10 hypercasual publishers for Q3 2025 exists but its contents were not retrieved. — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-guides/top-10-hypercasual-gaming-publishers-worldwide-q3-2025)

### Inferences
- "Revenue share on profit after UA" means a developer can ship a game with millions of installs and receive little, because the publisher controls UA spend and the accounting. With pay-per-prototype shrinking to hundreds of dollars, the publisher route is a lottery ticket with a low floor, not an income.
- For a developer in Brazil, $500 per prototype has more purchasing power than in the US or EU, but the hybrid-casual bar (deep playtime, 20% completion, meta systems) raises the cost of each prototype well above the weekend builds of 2019-2021.
- Publishers' public gates are useful even without signing: a solo developer can run the same CPI and D1 test for a few hundred dollars and kill ideas by the same rules.

### Gaps
- No hit rates (prototypes tested per published game) for any publisher; industry lore says well under 1 in 100, but I found no source this session.
- No current terms for Voodoo, SayGames, Azur, Rollic, CrazyLabs, Lion Studios, TapNation, Supercent or Habby; no minimum guarantees or advance sizes.
- No Brazilian or LATAM-specific publisher programme found (the queries for this did not run).

## 7. Platform and policy (Apple Arcade, mini-games, web shops, DMA, loot boxes, kids, Brazil)

### Takeaway
Platform economics moved in developers' favour in 2025-2026 (zero commission currently collected on US link-out purchases, 15% for mini-games, about 17-20% effective in the EU), though these gains matter mostly to games with real IAP volume. Regulation moved the other way for anything touching minors: Brazil's ECA Digital has banned loot boxes for under-18s since March 17 2026, and Apple's new 13+/16+/18+ ratings and US state age-assurance laws add compliance work.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple Arcade deal structure:** Apple pays upfront development funding plus ongoing "bonus pool" royalties tied to "qualifying sessions", a metric developers say is opaque; early (2019) advances were reported at $1-3M. — [MobileGamer.biz, "Inside Apple Arcade"](https://mobilegamer.biz/inside-apple-arcade-axed-games-declining-payouts-disillusioned-studios-and-an-uncertain-future/); [9to5Mac, 2019](https://9to5mac.com/2019/05/13/apple-arcade-developer-pitch/)
- **Apple Arcade trend (2024 reporting, anonymous developers):** upfront fees have been cut, bonus pool payouts have declined since about October 2020, projects were cancelled, payments ran up to five months late, and signing a deal is "very difficult and long"; "App Store Greats" ports get no upfront payment, only bonus pool. — [MobileGamer.biz, second report](https://mobilegamer.biz/inside-apple-arcade-again-late-payments-stonewalled-studios-terrible-tech-support-and-vision-pro-woes/); [MacRumors, Aug 2024](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/08/01/apple-arcade-frustrations-persist-for-developers/)
- **Mini-games:** Apple's Mini Apps Partner Program (announced November 13 2025) gives a 15% commission on qualifying IAP in mini apps and mini games; hosts must apply and support the Declared Age Range API and Advanced Commerce API; guideline 4.7 now explicitly covers HTML5/JavaScript mini apps and mini games, which must run in WebKit without native API access. — [Apple Developer news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=xcz1s7cz); [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/apple-mini-apps-partner-program.md); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/apple-launches-mini-apps-partner-program-with-reduced-commission-on-qualifying-in-app-purchases)
- **US web shops after Epic v. Apple:** the 2025 injunction bars Apple from charging commission on US external-link purchases; in December 2025 the Ninth Circuit held the total fee ban overbroad and remanded for a "reasonable" fee that has not been set; as of July 2026 no commission is being collected on US link-outs. Apple may keep external links from being more prominent than its own buttons, but must allow equal prominence. — [Unity blog, Jul 2026](https://unity.com/blog/mobile-game-webshop-business-case); [Neon, Dec 2025](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/the-latest-epic-v.-apple-appeal-ruling-december-2025-what-changed-for-game-developers)
- Unity's blog states the Supreme Court granted cert on June 30 2026 (No. 25-1311); single source, not independently confirmed. — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/mobile-game-webshop-business-case)
- **Google:** has announced an intended fee of up to about 20% on US external links that is not yet in effect. — [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/mobile-game-webshop-business-case)
- **EU (DMA):** Apple's fees still apply; a 5% Core Technology Commission plus other components stack to roughly 17-20% effective. — [Neon, Sep 2025](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/making-sense-of-apple-s-eu-app-store-terms-why-they-still-undermine-the-dma)
- **Web shop adoption (vendor data):** more than 60 of the top 100 grossing mobile games run a web shop; direct-to-consumer payments grew about 46% in 2025 (Unity cites 38% among the top 100); one vendor estimates about $0.92 net per dollar via web shop vs $0.70 via the App Store. Epic launched "webshops" for iOS developers (reported July 2026). — [Game Growth Advisor, May 2026](https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-05-05-mobile-game-d2c-web-shop-strategy-2026/); [Unity blog](https://unity.com/blog/mobile-game-webshop-business-case); [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/97187/epic-games-unveils-webshops-to-help-ios-developers-bypass-app-store-fees)
- **Brazil, ECA Digital (Lei 15.211/2025):** in force since March 17 2026; Art. 20 prohibits loot boxes in games aimed at, or likely to be accessed by, children and adolescents; regulating decree no. 12.880/2026 lets a game offer a version without loot boxes or disable them by default, in which case age verification is waived; ANPD enforces. — [Mattos Filho](https://www.mattosfilho.com.br/unico/eca-digital-sancionada-lei/); [Machado Meyer](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/direito-digital/estatuto-digital-da-crianca-e-do-adolescente-lei-n-15-211-2025-entra-em-vigor-em-17-de-marco-de-2026); [Agencia Gov](https://agenciagov.ebc.com.br/noticias/202603/lula-assina-decretos-que-regulamentam-eca-digital-conheca-em-10-pontos-a-nova-lei)
- ECA Digital in practice: the law also restricts player chat for minors; six titles had their age classification changed over loot boxes (EA Sports FC 26, Roblox, NBA 2K26, MLB The Show 25 and 26, WWE 2K26); the Justice Ministry asked ANPD to monitor games with loot boxes and no age verification. - **Apple age ratings:** new 13+, 16+ and 18+ tiers alongside 4+ and 9+ (announced July 2025); developers had to answer the updated questionnaire by January 31 2026 or have updates blocked. — [9to5Mac, Jul 24 2025](https://9to5mac.com/2025/07/24/apple-notifies-developers-of-new-app-store-age-rating-system/)
- **US state app-store age laws:** Texas, Utah and Louisiana passed App Store Accountability Acts requiring age categories (under 13, 13-15, 16-17, 18+) and age ratings for apps and in-app purchases; a federal judge enjoined the Texas law on December 23 2025 before its January 1 2026 start. Current status not checked. — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/fr-FR/Insights/2025/07/Texas-and-Louisiana-Join-Growing-Trend-of-State-Age-Verification-Laws-for-App-Stores); [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/an-app-developers-guide-to-app-store-age-assurance-laws/)

### Inferences
- Web shops and link-outs help games with meaningful IAP from repeat spenders. For a small ad-monetised or premium game the saving is small in absolute terms and adds payment, tax and support overhead; a small-volume game is better served by the standard store flow.
- The US zero-commission window is legally unstable (remand for a "reasonable" fee, possible Supreme Court review), so it should not be the basis of a business plan.

- Apple Arcade is best treated as an upside option to pitch, not as a plan.

## 8. Honest comparison: games vs subscription utility apps for a small team

### Takeaway
On base rates a mobile game is the higher-variance, lower-expected-value bet for a 1-3 person team: three quarters of games retain under 3% of players at day 28 and the money sits with 135 titles, whereas a subscription app has a measurable 17% chance of reaching $1K MRR within two years. Games still make sense in a few shapes: ad-monetised casual with unusually strong retention and organic installs, niche premium with an existing audience, daily puzzles with a habit loop, and games that monetise by subscription (the RevenueCat gaming category does best of all at the $10K MRR level).

### Cited Findings
- Subscription apps (RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026; 115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue): 17% of new apps reach $1,000 MRR within their first two years, down from 19% the year before; the share reaching $10,000 MRR fell from 5.3% to 4.6%. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/); [SaaStr summary](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/)
- Within that dataset, gaming is the best-performing category at the $10K level (9% of gaming apps reach $10K MRR) and the fastest to $1K MRR (median 32 days vs 113 days for business apps). — [RevenueCat insights](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-26-insights); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/the-app-middle-class-is-dying-and-revenuecats-data-shows-exactly-how-fast/)
- Subscription growth is concentrating: the median app's MRR grew 5.3% YoY, the top quartile grew 80%+, the bottom quartile shrank by more than 33%. — [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top)
- Games base rates (GameAnalytics): 75% of games have D28 retention under 3%; median D1 about 22% and D7 about 4%; retention fell again in 2025. — [GameAnalytics 2025 benchmarks (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-GameAnalytics-Mobile-Gaming-Benchmarks.pdf); [GameDev Reports on the 2026 edition](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)
- Games revenue concentration: 135 titles above $100M took $50.8B of about $82B IAP in 2025; non-game apps passed games in consumer spend in 2025; game downloads fell 7% in 2025 and 12% in H1 2026. — [Mobile Marketing Reads](https://mobilemarketingreads.com/?p=50460); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/?p=315027); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/mobile-gaming-iap-dipped-2-as-ad-spend-climbed-in-h1-2026/)

### Inferences
- The RevenueCat numbers are for apps that already have subscription revenue and use RevenueCat, so 17% overstates the odds for an arbitrary new app. No equivalent "share of new mobile games reaching $1K a month" statistic was found, so the comparison is base rates on different denominators: it supports "games are riskier", not a precise ratio.
- The RevenueCat gaming result is the most actionable finding for games: the games that do well for small teams in that dataset charge a subscription or recurring purchase, which removes dependence on ad eCPMs and on volume. A daily puzzle or a card/solitaire game for adults with a "no ads + extras" subscription fits this, and fits iOS, where 64% of game IAP sits.
- Why games are structurally harder: revenue per user is 10-100x lower than a subscription app (cents of ad revenue vs dollars per month), so a game needs 10-100x the audience, and audience is exactly what a small team cannot buy at $5-10 per US install.
- What games offer that utilities do not: faster signal (a CPI and D1 test in days for a few hundred dollars), a prototype market (publishers), and uncapped upside from virality. That suits a portfolio approach of many cheap tests with strict kill rules better than a single long build.
- Reasonable positioning for a 1-3 person team in 2026: treat games as a small, time-boxed share of the portfolio; choose genres with habit retention and low content cost (daily/word puzzle, card, solitaire-style for older players); monetise with subscription or remove-ads plus light ads; launch on iOS in tier-1 markets; rely on organic, web and social distribution rather than paid UA; avoid loot boxes and child-directed designs.

### Gaps
- No sourced probability that a new mobile game is profitable, or share of games reaching a revenue threshold. Statements such as "under 1% of mobile games are profitable" circulate without a primary source and should not be used.
- No sourced LTV or ARPU for subscription-monetised games vs ad-monetised games, and no retention data for games aimed at older audiences.
- Solo-developer income distributions on mobile (as opposed to Steam) were not found.
- RevenueCat's year labels for the 19% and 17% cohorts differ between summaries; check the report before quoting years.
