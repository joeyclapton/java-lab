## 1. Total consumer spending: iOS App Store vs Google Play in 2024, 2025, 2026 YTD; games vs non-games; when non-games overtook games

### Takeaway
2025 was the first full year in which non-game apps out-earned games across the two stores combined (Sensor Tower: $85.6B vs $81.8B of a ~$167B total; Appfigures: $82.6B vs $72.2B of $155.8B), and iOS carries roughly 70% of store spend (~$117.6B vs ~$49.2B on Google Play). The trackers disagree on levels and growth rates (Sensor Tower +10.6% total vs Appfigures +21.6%), and no reliable total for 2026 year-to-date was found; the only 2026 data point is that store-measured games revenue is now shrinking (Q3 2026: -6.9% YoY).

### Inferences
- Implied 2024 baselines from Sensor Tower's 2025 growth rates: total ≈ $151B (167/1.106); non-games ≈ $70.7B (85.6/1.21); games ≈ $81.0B (81.8/1.01).
- Implied iOS 2024 ≈ $103.5B (117.6/1.136); implied Google Play 2024 ≈ $47.5B (151 − 103.5), i.e. Google Play grew only ~3–4% in 2025 versus ~13.6% for iOS. This mixes two secondary sources, so treat as approximate.
- iOS non-game spend 2025 ≈ $65B (117.6 − 52.5) = ~55% of iOS spend; Google Play non-game ≈ $19B (49.2 − 30) = ~39% of Play spend. For a non-game subscription app, the iOS revenue pool is roughly 3.4x the Google Play pool.
- On iOS specifically, non-games were therefore clearly ahead of games in full-year 2025 (~$65B vs ~$52B), a wider lead than in the combined-store numbers, because Google Play remains games-heavy (~61% games).
- The Sensor Tower/Appfigures gap (+10.6% vs +21.6% total growth; games $81.8B vs $72.2B) is large enough that growth rates should always be quoted with the tracker named. Direction is consistent; magnitude is not.
- From mid-2025 on, store-measured revenue increasingly understates real consumer spend in the US because of zero-commission external links (see section 5); 2026 "declines" in store data partly reflect leakage to web checkout rather than falling demand.

### Gaps
- No total (games + non-games) consumer-spend figure for H1 2026 or Q1–Q3 2026 was found for either store; Sensor Tower/Appfigures quarterly indexes for 2026 need to be pulled directly.
- Aggregator blogs quote 2026 full-year projections from "$190–220B" up to "$233B" attributed to Sensor Tower; these are unsourced/contradictory and were not used.
- A downloads total of "142.2B in 2025, +3.1%" appears in one aggregator and conflicts with Sensor Tower's +0.8%; source not identified.
- Whether Appfigures' $155.8B is gross or net of store fees was not confirmed.
- UNVERIFIED RECOLLECTION: Sensor Tower's State of Mobile 2025 (Jan 2025) reported 2024 IAP revenue of ~$150B (+12.5% YoY), games ~$80.9B (+4%) and non-games ~$69.2B (+25%). These match the implied baselines above but were not re-checked.
- UNVERIFIED RECOLLECTION: on the **US App Store alone**, Sensor Tower reported non-game spending overtaking games in Q2 2022 (first month: May 2022, ~50.3% share). If confirmed, the iOS-US crossover came about three years before the global, all-store crossover in Q2 2025. I found no dated source this session for the global iOS-only crossover.

## 2. Revenue and download ranking by App Store category; fastest-growing categories 2025–2026; size of the "AI apps" segment

### Takeaway
Generative AI is the fastest-growing non-game category by a wide margin (~$5B of store spend in 2025, roughly tripling YoY, forecast >$10B in 2026) but is extremely concentrated (ChatGPT alone ≈ $3.4B); Social Media, Streaming video and Dating remain the largest non-game revenue pools. For small subscription developers, the benchmark datasets point to Health & Fitness, Utilities and Photo & Video as the categories with the best per-user economics or hit rates, while AI companions are a real but small niche (~$327M in H1 2026).

## 3. Subscription and IAP benchmarks (RevenueCat 2025/2026, Adapty, Superwall, Appfigures, Sensor Tower)

### Takeaway
The median subscription app monetizes installs poorly: RevenueCat 2026 puts median revenue per install at day 60 at $0.38 for freemium apps versus $3.09 for hard-paywall apps, and $0.55 overall in North America versus $0.11 in India/SE Asia. Weekly plans now produce the majority of revenue in Adapty's dataset (55.5%) but retain worst; annual plans retain best, yet ~35% of annual cancellations happen in the first month and 95% of cancelled annual subscribers never return.

### Cited Findings
**RevenueCat, State of Subscription Apps 2026 (published early March 2026; dataset: 115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue, 1B+ transactions; 2025 data)**
- Dataset size — [SaaStr summary](https://saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps-how-115000-mobile-apps-deliver-16b-in-revenue-whats-working-whats-quietly-killing-growth); [RevenueCat report page](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- Paywall model: hard-paywall apps have a median Day-35 conversion of 10.7% vs 2.1% for freemium apps (the snippet labels this "trial-to-paid"; the magnitudes and RevenueCat's prior editions indicate it is download-to-paid by day 35 — verify label) — [RevenueCat blog, "State of Subscription Apps in 10 minutes"](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Top 10% of hard-paywall apps reach 38.7% conversion — [ARPU Brothers summary](https://arpubrothers.com/blog/revenuecat-subscription-app-report-2026/)
- Revenue per install (RPI), median: Day 14 = $2.32 hard paywall vs $0.27 freemium; Day 60 = $3.09 hard paywall vs $0.38 freemium — [RevenueCat blog](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)

### Inferences
- Order-of-magnitude funnel for a freemium app in a tier-1 market, combining the two datasets: ~10–15% install-to-trial (Adapty NA 14.5%) × ~25–40% trial-to-paid (RevenueCat by trial length) ≈ 2.5–6% install-to-paid for trial-led apps, which brackets RevenueCat's 2.1% freemium median (many freemium apps show the paywall to only part of the base) and sits far below the 10.7% hard-paywall median.
- Gross revenue per 1,000 installs at the median (RevenueCat D60): ≈ $380 freemium, ≈ $3,090 hard paywall; after Apple's 15% Small Business rate that is ≈ $323 and ≈ $2,627. A hard paywall's higher RPI is partly selection (hard-paywall apps tend to buy high-intent traffic and get fewer organic installs), so it should not be read as "switching paywall type multiplies revenue 8x".
- Geography moves RPI by ~5x (NA $0.55 vs IN/SEA $0.11 at D60), i.e. more than paywall copy or pricing tests typically do; market choice is a first-order modelling input.
- Plan-mix trade-off: weekly-with-trial maximizes 12-month LTV per subscriber in Adapty's data ($49–55) but only ~5.5% survive to day 380, so revenue depends on continuous acquisition; annual gives cash up front and ~20–31% year-2 renewal. For a solo developer with organic traffic, annual + monthly is the lower-churn base; weekly is the paid-UA play.
- First-year annual renewal should be modelled at roughly 20–31% (Adapty 19.9% at D380 for trial cohorts; RevenueCat 30.7% non-AI, 21.1% AI), monthly 12-month survival at roughly 6–14%.
- Because one-third of conversions happen on install day and >60% within week one, D7–D14 RPI is a usable early proxy for D60 (RevenueCat medians imply D60 ≈ 1.33–1.41x D14).

## 4. Revenue distribution among apps: who reaches $1k / $10k / $50k / $100k+ MRR; median revenue; concentration

## 5. Platform commission structures in 2026 (Apple: US, EU, Japan, Brazil; Google Play) and what web checkout saves in practice

### Takeaway
Apple's headline rates are unchanged globally (30% standard; 15% under the Small Business Program and after a subscriber's first year), but the effective rate now depends on storefront: in the US, purchases via in-app external links currently carry no Apple commission while a court decides whether to allow Apple's proposed 15%/5% link-out fee; the EU moved to a 26/20/15/5 rate card on 1 Oct 2026; Japan and Brazil have similar tiered regimes. Google Play cut to a 10% service fee (first $1M and all subscriptions) plus a 5% billing fee in the US/UK/EEA from 30 Jun 2026. Web checkout saves fees but the one public controlled test showed lower conversion wiping out the gain (≈ $0.93 per $1.00 of IAP revenue).

### Cited Findings
**Apple baseline**
- Standard In-App Purchase commission is 30% in the first year; 15% after a subscriber's first year or from the start under the Small Business Program — [Launch with Voyage guide](https://www.launchwithvoyage.com/guides/app-store-fees-vs-web-checkout)
- The Small Business Program covers developers earning less than US$1M annually — [MacRumors, 17 Dec 2025](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/12/17/japan-app-store-feature-updates)
- Apple (Analysis Group study) says more than 90% of the $1.4T ecosystem billings and sales in 2025 incurred no commission — [Apple Newsroom, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/app-store-ecosystem-reaches-1-point-4-trillion-usd-as-developers-thrive-globally/)

**United States (Epic v. Apple anti-steering)**
- An April 2025 federal ruling barred Apple from charging any commission on purchases made through external payment links in US apps; Apple's earlier 27% link-out commission was found non-compliant — [RevenueCat, "Apple must allow External Payment Links"](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-anti-steering-ruling-monetization-strategy)

- Apple's proposal to the district court (13–14 Aug 2026): up to 15% on link-out purchases for standard apps and 5% for Small Business Program apps; Epic says the fees are "far outside of the bounds" of the court's guidance and will contest any rate above zero — [TechCrunch, 14 Aug 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/apple-proposes-to-take-a-15-cut-of-purchases-made-outside-the-app-store/); [AppleInsider, 13 Aug 2026](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/08/13/apples-latest-commission-rates-for-external-app-store-purchases-havent-satisfied-epic)

**What web checkout saves in practice**
- RevenueCat controlled test on the Dipsea app (US, 2025; single app, described as preliminary): IAP-only arm 27.0% started a trial vs 18.1% in the web-checkout-only arm; offering web brought in $0.93 for every $1.00 earned through IAP even after counting the full App Store fee saving (~6–7% lower take-home) — [RevenueCat, "Web vs. in-app subscriptions"](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/iap-vs-web-purchases-conversion-test)
- An earlier test found most of the drop occurs between the payment sheet and purchase completion — [Pixel Envy](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/in-app-purchase-conversion-rate/)
- Other guides quote 10–20% lower conversion for external checkout vs Apple IAP (no test cited) and recommend a hybrid: IAP for low-ticket monthly plans, web for high-ticket/annual bundles — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/does-web-to-app-improve-profitability); [Passion.io](https://passion.io/blog/in-app-purchases-for-community-apps-fees-setup-guide)
- Stripe markets web payments as "up to 90% lower" fees than app-store commissions (vendor claim) — [Stripe](https://stripe.com/en-hr/use-cases/in-app-payments)

### Inferences
- Break-even arithmetic for a US link-out today (0% Apple fee, ~3% processor): a Small Business Program developer keeps ~$0.97 vs $0.85 per dollar, so web wins only if it loses less than ~12% of conversions; a 30%-tier developer keeps ~$0.97 vs $0.70 and can lose up to ~28%. The RevenueCat test saw ~33% fewer trial starts on web-only (18.1% vs 27.0%), which is why it netted negative.
- If the court approves Apple's proposed 15%/5% link-out fee, the margin shrinks to ~$0.92 vs $0.85 for small developers (break-even at ~8% conversion loss) and ~$0.82 vs $0.70 for standard-rate developers (~15%). For a sub-$1M developer the fee saving is then too small to justify the conversion risk plus the tax/merchant-of-record burden.
- For developers already at 15%, the practical value of web flows is not the fee but (a) web-to-app acquisition funnels with ad-platform attribution, (b) owning the billing relationship, and (c) faster payouts; the fee argument mainly applies above $1M/year or to high-ticket annual plans.
- Effective take rates for a sub-$1M subscription developer in Nov 2026: Apple worldwide 15% (EU reported 10–15%, Japan 10% + 5% IAP = 15%, Brazil 10% + 5% = 15%); Google Play 15% in US/UK/EEA via Play Billing (10% + 5%) and, on the legacy model, 15% elsewhere until the staged rollout completes. The platforms have converged on ~15% all-in for small developers; differences now matter mostly above $1M.
- A Brazil-based developer selling in Brazil gets the new Apple options (link-out at 15%, alternative processing) but they are not cheaper than the 15% Small Business rate, so they do not change small-developer economics.

### Gaps
- Apple's official pages (developer.apple.com: Small Business Program, US storefront external links, EU terms, Japan/Brazil terms) and Google's Play Console "Service fees" help page could not be opened; all rates above are from press and secondary guides and several conflict (EU small-developer IAP rate; Japan vs Korea rollout date for Google; Brazil announcement date).
- No final ruling on the US link-out commission was found; outcome of the remand and of the Supreme Court case is pending as of 13 Nov 2026.
- Whether the Oct 1, 2026 EU terms went live exactly as announced was not confirmed by a post-October source.
- No data found on changes in other markets (UK, South Korea, Australia, China) for Apple, nor on adoption rates of US external links among subscription apps in 2026.
- No 2026 multi-app study of web-checkout conversion was found; evidence rests on one RevenueCat test from 2025.
- UNVERIFIED RECOLLECTION: Google Play's legacy model (still applying outside the rollout markets until 2026–2027) is 15% on the first $1M/year and on subscriptions, 30% above. Typical web payment costs are ~2.9% + $0.30 (Stripe, US cards) or ~5% + $0.50 for a merchant-of-record such as Paddle/Lemon Squeezy, which also handles VAT/sales tax.

### Inferences
- US share of global non-game store spend ≈ 41% (33.6/82.6, Appfigures) and ≈ 36% of all store spend (60/167, Sensor Tower); a non-game app's revenue is therefore more US-weighted than the market overall.
- Brazil's Apple-reported iOS digital-goods billings (~$1.4B) are ~2% of the US figure (~$62B) and under 1% of Apple's global digital goods and services ($149B). A Brazil-only iOS subscription app addresses a pool roughly 40–45x smaller than the US one before considering lower price points.
- Combining Adapty (install-to-trial ~1.5–1.9x higher in NA) and RevenueCat (D60 RPI 5x NA vs IN/SEA), most of the regional gap comes from price and conversion together; LATAM likely sits between Europe and IN/SEA, but no LATAM-specific benchmark was retrieved.
- English-language, US-first launch with localized pricing tiers (index ~1.2 for big-5 Europe, deep discounts for Turkey-type markets) is what the benchmark data supports for revenue; home-market-only launch in Brazil is a volume/learning play, more naturally on Android.

## 7. Ad monetization baseline for non-game apps (eCPM by format, tier-1 vs LATAM)

### Takeaway
Published eCPM benchmarks are dominated by gaming data and vary by more than 2x between sources: US rewarded video is quoted at roughly $13–30 and US interstitials at ~$14–23 in game datasets, while generalist guides give much lower "typical" iOS ranges ($3–8 interstitial, $0.50–2.50 banner). Latin America is quoted at single-digit dollars (≈ $3.4 Android rewarded, ≈ $1.8 iOS interstitial), i.e. roughly 5–10x below the US; no reliable non-gaming or Brazil-specific series was found.

### Cited Findings
- US rewarded video eCPM ranges from ~$13 to ~$30 depending on the source (a >2x spread) — [Playio, Rewarded ad benchmarks 2026](https://blog.playio.co/rewarded-ad-benchmarks-2026)
- US iOS (mobile-game dataset, 2026): rewarded ≈ $24.39, interstitial ≈ $23.17 — [Playio, Mobile game eCPM benchmarks 2026](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)
- Rewarded video ≈ $16.49 on Android and ≈ $19.63 on iOS; interstitials ≈ $14 on both (US) — [AppLixir](https://www.applixir.com/?p=4289)
- "Typical" ranges in a generalist 2026 guide: iOS interstitial $3.00–8.00; iOS banner $0.50–2.50 — [MonetizeMore](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/how-much-ad-revenue-can-apps-generate/)
- Non-gaming-oriented block: US banner $0.50–2.00 (layout of the source is ambiguous) — [Appalize](https://www.appalize.com/blog/monetization/admob-vs-alternatives-maximizing-your-ad-revenue)
- Older data: US banner eCPM $0.60 on Android and $0.27 on iOS, the highest of any country in that dataset — [Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-apps/ecpms)
- Latin America (game dataset; table partly garbled in the snippet): Android rewarded ≈ $3.4; iOS interstitial ≈ $1.8, well below North America; Brazil is not broken out — [Playio, Mobile game eCPM benchmarks 2026](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)
- Scale context: in-app advertising accounted for $151B of the App Store ecosystem's billings and sales in 2025 (Apple/Analysis Group; all advertisers, dominated by large platforms) — [Apple Newsroom, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/06/app-store-ecosystem-reaches-1-point-4-trillion-usd-as-developers-thrive-globally/)
- Appodeal publishes quarterly country-level eCPM reports (Q4 2024 edition located, not read) — [Appodeal Q4 2024 eCPM report (PDF)](https://appodeal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Quarterly_Mobile_eCPM_Report_-_Q4_2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- Illustrative ad ARPDAU for a non-game utility in the US using the conservative generalist ranges: 2 interstitials/day at $5 eCPM ($0.010) + 10 banner impressions at $1 eCPM ($0.010) ≈ $0.02 per DAU per day, i.e. ≈ $0.60 per fully-active user per month; with LATAM eCPMs 5–10x lower this becomes ≈ $0.06–0.12. This is my arithmetic on the ranges above, not a sourced benchmark.
- Against RevenueCat's freemium median of $0.38 gross revenue per install at day 60, ads only beat subscriptions when retention is high and sessions are frequent (habit utilities, content, tools used daily); for low-frequency apps a paywall dominates.
- In Brazil/LATAM the ratio flips relative to tier-1: subscription willingness-to-pay is low and eCPMs are low, so ad-supported apps need very large DAU; a hybrid (ads for the free tier, a cheap "remove ads" or weekly plan) is the usual compromise.
- Rewarded formats carry the highest eCPMs but need a value exchange that most non-game apps lack; interstitial and banner ranges are the realistic baseline for utilities.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–2026 eCPM series for non-gaming apps by format was found; almost all published benchmarks are gaming or vendor blogs with unclear samples.
- No Brazil-specific eCPM by format; LATAM figures come from one partly garbled table.
- No fill-rate, impressions-per-DAU or ad-ARPDAU benchmarks for non-game categories were retrieved.
- Appodeal/AdMob country-level reports for 2025–2026 could not be opened.

## 8. Apple-reported figures: ecosystem billings, developer payouts, number of apps, developers above earnings thresholds

### Takeaway
Apple's latest self-reported number (4 Jun 2026, Analysis Group study it commissioned) is $1.4 trillion of developer billings and sales facilitated by the App Store ecosystem in 2025, of which only $149B is digital goods and services; $1.1T is physical goods/services and $151B in-app advertising. Apple-reported counts of apps, developers and payout totals for 2026 were not retrieved in this session.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Apple's $149B "digital goods and services" for 2025 is larger than third-party iOS consumer-spend estimates (~$117.6B) because it includes digital sales that happen outside Apple's IAP (e.g. subscriptions bought on the web and consumed in the app); the gap (~$31B) is a rough indication of how much iOS digital revenue already bypasses IAP.
- Digital goods are only ~10.6% of Apple's headline number (149/1,400); the figure relevant to an IAP/subscription developer is the ~$117–149B digital pool, not $1.4T.
- If the commissionable base is roughly the third-party IAP estimate (~$117.6B) and Apple's blended take is 15–30%, Apple's App Store commission revenue is on the order of $20–30B per year; this is my estimate, not an Apple figure.
